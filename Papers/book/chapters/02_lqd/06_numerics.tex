\section{Numerical realization}
\label{sec:numerics}

The mathematical model lives on the whole log-odds line.  A computer stores a
finite central grid, so the two tails must be continued analytically.  The
important design rule is consistency: normalization, strike roots, prices,
and calendar tests must all use the same continuation.  One slice build then
produces the transport, density ingredients, ledger, and sensitivity arrays.

\subsection{The central grid}

Use a symmetric grid $[-Z_{\max},Z_{\max}]$.  At each node compute
$u=\Lambda(z)$, the two gauges \eqref{eq:ellgauges}, $g(u)$, and $x'(z)$ from
\eqref{eq:xspeed}.  Cumulative quadrature anchored at $z=0$ gives
$\bar x=x-m$.  The martingale integral gives $m$.  Reverse cumulative
quadrature gives $G$.

The reference sizes are 2001 nodes during optimization and 8001 nodes for the
accepted slice, with $Z_{\max}=40$.  Cubic Hermite interpolation uses the exact
nodal derivatives
\begin{equation}
x'(z)>0,
\qquad G'(z)=-e^{x(z)}\rho(z)<0.
\label{eq:nodalderivatives}
\end{equation}
A bracketed Hermite root followed by one Newton step gives $z_k$.

Three expressions need special numerical treatment.  First,
\[
\rho(z)=\expit(z)\expit(-z),
\]
because $1-\expit(z)$ rounds to zero in the positive tail.  Second, the cash
leg is
\[
e^k(1-\Lambda(z_k))
=\exp\{k-\log(1+e^{z_k})\},
\]
evaluated with a log-sum-exp routine.  Third, trial points are rejected before
exponentiation if any grid value of $g$, $x$, or a sensitivity exponent exceeds
the floating-point budget.

\subsection{Tail continuation}

For $z>Z_{\max}$, the leading transport continuation is
\begin{equation}
x_R(z)=x(Z_{\max})+
\frac{\lambda_+}{1-\alpha_+}
\left[(z+1)^{1-\alpha_+}
-(Z_{\max}+1)^{1-\alpha_+}\right].
\label{eq:rightcontinuation}
\end{equation}
The corresponding approximate root for $k>x(Z_{\max})$ is
\begin{equation}
z_R(k)+1=\left[(Z_{\max}+1)^{1-\alpha_+}
+\frac{(1-\alpha_+)(k-x(Z_{\max}))}{\lambda_+}
\right]^{1/(1-\alpha_+)}.
\label{eq:rightroot}
\end{equation}
The formulas reduce to a straight line when $\alpha_+=0$.  The left
continuation is obtained by replacing $z+1$ by $1-z$ and reversing the sign of
the transport increment.

In the exponential subclass, the leading missing martingale masses are
\begin{equation}
\int_{Z_{\max}}^\infty e^{\bar x}\rho\,\dd z
\simeq\frac{e^{\bar x(Z_{\max})-Z_{\max}}}{1-\lambda_+},
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{-Z_{\max}} e^{\bar x}\rho\,\dd z
\simeq\frac{e^{\bar x(-Z_{\max})-Z_{\max}}}{1+\lambda_-}.
\label{eq:tailcorr}
\end{equation}
For $\alpha_+>0$, the right correction is evaluated by one-dimensional
log-domain quadrature under \eqref{eq:rightcontinuation}.  Its endpoint
approximation is
\begin{equation}
\int_{Z_{\max}}^\infty e^{x(z)}\rho(z)\,\dd z
\simeq
\frac{e^{x(Z_{\max})-Z_{\max}}}{1-x'(Z_{\max})},
\label{eq:lighttailcorr}
\end{equation}
provided $x'(Z_{\max})<1$.  The left denominator is
$1+x'(-Z_{\max})$.

The condition in \eqref{eq:lighttailcorr} is numerical, not probabilistic.  A
positive tail exponent guarantees a finite forward, yet the normalizing
integrand can first grow and only decay at an extremely remote rank.  More
precisely, if $x'(Z_{\max})>1$, it grows until the saddle $x'(z)=1$.  A
production implementation must either locate that saddle and use scaled
quadrature around it, or enforce the operational guard
\begin{equation}
x'(Z_{\max})\le1-\varepsilon_{\rm tail}.
\label{eq:operationaltailguard}
\end{equation}
The reference implementation uses the guard.  It excludes laws whose forward
is finite in theory but numerically determined by ranks far beyond the grid.

For a strike beyond the right grid, evaluate the ledger under the same tail
continuation and subtract the logistic cash leg.  In the far limit,
\begin{equation}
c(k)\simeq e^{k-z_R(k)}
\frac{x'(z_R(k))}{1-x'(z_R(k))}.
\label{eq:beyondgrid}
\end{equation}
At $\alpha_+=0$, this becomes
$e^{k-z_R(k)}\lambda_+/(1-\lambda_+)$.  Deep left calls are evaluated as a
small put plus parity, avoiding subtraction of two numbers close to one.

\subsection{Numerical certificates}
\label{sec:certificates}

The continuous formulas are arbitrage-free; a poorly resolved interpolation
can still violate their monotonicity on a computer.  Four independent checks
are applied to every accepted slice.

\begin{enumerate}
\item On every cubic Hermite segment for $x$, verify the Fritsch--Carlson box
\[
0\le d_0\le3s,\qquad 0\le d_1\le3s,
\]
where $s$ is the secant slope and $d_0,d_1$ are endpoint derivatives.  Apply
the same check to $-G$ \citep{FritschCarlson1980}.
\item Across the retained strike range, compute analytic $w,w',w''$ and
insert them in \eqref{eq:durrleman}.  Require
$g_{\rm D}\ge-\varepsilon_{\rm dens}$, where the tolerance is stated in units
of the local Black density factor.
\item Rebuild on a grid with four times as many nodes.  Compare prices,
densities, ATM handles, and the martingale integral at off-grid strikes and
ranks.
\item Test tail formulas separately in the exponential logistic reference
law and in a Gaussian-rate reference obtained from
$X\sim N(-s^2/2,s^2)$ with $\alpha_\pm=1/2$.
\end{enumerate}

The Hermite box is sufficient rather than necessary.  Its smallest remaining
margin detects under-resolution before monotonicity is visibly lost.

The accepted parameter vector is rebuilt once on the publication grid.
Prices, densities, ATM handles, variance-swap values, and calendar ledgers all
read that same immutable object.  Quote residuals measure market fit; basis
order and tail exponents record modeling choices; numerical margins measure
the reliability of the stored representation.  They are reported separately.
