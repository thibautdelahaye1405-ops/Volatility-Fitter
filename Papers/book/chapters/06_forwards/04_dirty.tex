\section{Estimating on a dirty chain}
\label{sec:fwdtrim}

The noise model of \cref{sec:fwdident} was polite: every quote wrong
by a little, none wrong by a lot.  Real chains are not polite.  A
quote can be \emph{stale} --- last updated minutes ago, while the
market moved --- or \emph{crossed} --- its bid above its ask, a pair
no live market would let stand --- or simply wrong, and one such
point is not two cents off the line but a dollar off.  This section adds the
classical defense, watches it work, and then --- more important ---
finds its blind spot.

The defense is an \emph{iterated trim}, and it is standard robust
statistics rather than anything of ours \citep{RousseeuwLeroy1987}.
Fit the line.  Compute the residuals.  Estimate their typical size
robustly --- take the median of the absolute residuals and multiply by
$1.4826$, the constant that makes this estimator agree with the
standard deviation on clean Gaussian data, so one huge outlier cannot
inflate the scale used to judge it; floor the estimate at one basis
point of spot so a suspiciously perfect board does not set an
impossibly strict bar.  Call the result $\hat\sigma$.  Drop every
pair whose residual exceeds $4\hat\sigma$, refit, and repeat --- at
most three rounds, and never below three surviving pairs, since two
points can always draw a perfect line.  On a clean board the loop
finds nothing, drops nothing, and returns the plain fit bit for bit;
the defense costs nothing when it is not needed.

\Cref{fig:fwdtrim} stages the intended failure on the running board.
One deep put, at strike 75, is stale: its quoted mid is too high by
\$\MacFwdTrimStaleDollars, so $\Pi(75)$ sits
\$\MacFwdTrimStaleDollars\ below the line.  In
panel~(a) the corrupted point is ringed; the fitted line shown is the
trimmed one, and the eye confirms there was never any doubt about
where the line should go.  Panel~(b) shows the residuals against that
trimmed fit, with the $\pm4\hat\sigma$ band shaded: every clean point
lives inside a band a few cents tall, and the stale put stands roughly
\MacFwdTrimSigmaOut\ robust sigmas outside it.  It is dropped in the
first round (\MacFwdTrimNOut\ point trimmed), and the refit recovers
the true forward to \MacFwdTrimErrBp\,bp.

The raw fit's damage, before the trim, is itself a lesson in
\cref{sec:fwdident}'s asymmetry.  The untrimmed line's forward is off
by only \MacFwdTrimRawErrBp\,bp --- one gross point among
\MacFwdIdentNStrikes\ barely moves the level --- but its implied rate
comes out at \MacFwdTrimRawRatePct\%\ against a true $4\%$.  Even vandalism obeys the proposition: the damage
concentrates in the slope.

\begin{remark}[what the trim cannot see: coherent staleness]
\label{rem:fwdmask}
A residual trim detects points that disagree with the consensus line.
Stale points that agree \emph{with each other} are a different
animal.  Rerun the experiment with a coherent failure: instead of one
random error, let the \MacFwdMaskNWing\ lowest strikes --- the deep
put wing, exactly the quotes that update least often --- all go stale
\emph{together}, their prices recomputed at a financing rate
\MacFwdMaskDeltaRatePct\%\ away from the truth, as if that whole wing
last traded under yesterday's funding.  Each stale quote is now consistent with its stale
neighbors, the blended least-squares line splits the difference
between the two regimes, and the residuals spread out enough that
\emph{nothing} exceeds $4\hat\sigma$: the trim flags
\MacFwdMaskNOut\ points.  This is the \emph{masking} phenomenon of
robust statistics \citep{RousseeuwLeroy1987} --- contamination that
moves the consensus toward itself hides from any test built on
distance to the consensus.  The fitted rate lands at
\MacFwdMaskRatePct\%\ against a true $4\%$: badly wrong, yet entirely
plausible --- no test on the rate's \emph{value} would blink at it.  What survives is, once again, the level:
the forward moves only \MacFwdMaskFwdErrBp\,bp, because
\MacFwdMaskNWing\ stale quotes on one wing tilt the line without
lifting its center much.  Coherent staleness looks, from inside the
chain, exactly like a change in the financing rate; it cannot be
detected there, only bounded --- and the bound is
\cref{prop:fwdols}'s asymmetry itself.
\end{remark}

Where we stand: the trim removes quotes that betray themselves;
coherent staleness does not betray itself, corrupts the slope, and
spares the level.  So the slope needs protection that does not come
from the chain at all: prior knowledge of where a rate can plausibly
live.  The next section builds that defense --- and is honest about
its reach.  It stops slopes that leave the plausible range; the
masking experiment's slope, plausible by construction, is beyond any
within-chain repair, and stays bounded rather than fixed.
