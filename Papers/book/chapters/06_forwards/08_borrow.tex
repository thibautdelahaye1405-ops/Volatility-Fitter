\section{The residual rate: borrow, at the edge of identifiability}
\label{sec:fwdborrow}

The carry now has two named parts, rate and dividends.  On most
names they exhaust it.  On some they do not, and the leftover
has a name and a market of its own.

To sell a stock short, one must first \emph{borrow} the shares, and
the lender charges a fee.  For most large names the fee is a few
basis points and nobody prices it.  But when a stock is crowded on
the short side --- \emph{hard to borrow}, in desk language --- the
fee can run to hundreds or thousands of basis points a year, and it
enters the forward through the same replication logic as everything
else in this chapter --- from both sides.  The arbitrage that keeps
the forward from sitting too \emph{low} requires selling the stock
and buying it forward, and that arbitrageur pays the fee: the
forward can sink below its frictionless level by the fee before
pushing it back turns a profit.  And the fee pulls from above too:
whoever \emph{holds} the stock can lend it out and collect the same
fee, so holding shares unlent while the forward trades higher leaves
money on the table, and competition among lenders presses the
forward down to the fee-adjusted level.  (In practice lendable
supply and fee splits leave a band rather than a line; the equation
below reads as its middle.)  Writing $b$ for the borrow rate, the
carried forward becomes
\begin{equation}
F=S\,e^{(r-q_d-b)\,t}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
b=r-q_d-\frac1t\log\frac FS .
\label{eq:fwdborrowdef}
\end{equation}
Read the second form as a measurement recipe: given the rate, the
dividend model, and the parity-fitted forward, the borrow is the
carry \emph{residual} --- the rate left over when the named parts are
subtracted.  Nothing new has to be fitted; the chapter's line already
contains the number.  The question, in this chapter's spirit, is not
whether $b$ can be computed --- it always can --- but whether the
computation \emph{means} anything.  Two closed forms answer that, one
for the stakes and one for the precision, and the chapter has already
built both.

\emph{Materiality.}  A borrow error is a carry error, and a carry
error reaches the smile through the forward it misplaces:
by \eqref{eq:fwdborrowdef}, a shift $\delta b$ moves $\log F$ by
$-t\,\delta b$ --- the sign says a costlier borrow lowers the
forward; the size is what matters here --- and
\cref{sec:fwdskew}'s conversion \eqref{eq:fwddsig}
turns that into implied volatility.  At the money,
\begin{equation}
\bigg|\frac{\dd\sigma}{\dd b}\bigg|
=t\cdot\frac{\PhiN(d_+)}{\phin(d_+)\sqrt\tau}
\;\approx\;1.25\,\sqrt t
\qquad(\text{clocks agreeing, }d_+\text{ small}),
\label{eq:fwdbsens}
\end{equation}
about $125\sqrt t$ volatility basis points per hundred basis points
of borrow.  \Cref{fig:fwdborrow}(a) draws the exact curve at a flat
\MacFwdBorrowSigmaPct\% volatility: \MacFwdBorrowSensQBp\ vol bp at
three months, \MacFwdBorrowSensYBp\ at a year.  On a genuinely
hard-to-borrow name, the borrow is not a financing footnote; it is a
first-order input to the smile's \emph{level}, on par with the
volatility quotes themselves.

\emph{Precision.}  How well does the board pin the residual?  By
\cref{prop:fwdols}, the parity forward is known to about
$\varepsilon\sqrt2/\sqrt n$ in dollars --- the level line --- hence
to $\varepsilon\sqrt2/(\sqrt n\,S)$ as a relative error, taking
$DF\approx S$ for this back-of-envelope.  Inverting
\eqref{eq:fwdborrowdef}, a borrow perturbs the log-forward through
the factor $t$, so the forward's noise divides by $t$ on its way to
$b$: the smallest borrow the board can distinguish from zero is
\begin{equation}
b_{\min}
\;\approx\;\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt2}{\sqrt n\;S\;t}.
\label{eq:fwdfloor}
\end{equation}
This is the borrow's \emph{identifiability floor} --- one standard
error of the residual, so ``resolve'' here means the one-sigma
convention --- and its shape is the now-familiar $1/t$: precisely
the amplification that made short-dated parity rates meaningless in
\cref{sec:fwdident} makes short-dated borrow reads meaningless too.
\Cref{fig:fwdborrow}(b) draws \eqref{eq:fwdfloor} for a board sized
like a typical single name rather than the running board ---
\MacFwdBorrowNPairs\ pairs, quote noise stated in basis points of
spot; constants in appendix \ref{app:fwdprotocol} --- at two noise
levels, against the shaded range of borrow fees typical of genuinely
hard names.  Read it
maturity by maturity.  At one week, even a clean board resolves
nothing finer than \MacFwdBorrowFloorWeekBp\,bp --- larger than most
hard-to-borrow fees themselves, so a week-dated ``borrow read'' is
noise.  At three months and ten basis points of quote
noise the floor is \MacFwdBorrowFloorQBp\,bp; at a year it is
\MacFwdBorrowFloorYBp\,bp on a clean board and
\MacFwdBorrowFloorWideYBp\,bp on a noisy one --- comfortably below
the shaded band.  A borrow read is a measurement exactly where its
floor sits under the fee it measures; elsewhere it is a
guess in a measurement's format.

The tension between the two panels is the section's conclusion, and
it is a design principle rather than a formula.  Borrow matters most
at the short end of a stressed name --- exactly where
\eqref{eq:fwdfloor} says it cannot be measured from one chain.  The
correct output of an estimator in that position is not a number but a
declared state: \emph{unidentified}, with the floor
that justifies it --- never a silent zero, which is also a borrow
estimate, just an undeclared one.  The reference implementation
carries its carry as a per-expiry decomposition in which every leg is
tagged with its source --- measured, supplied, or unidentified ---
and lets a fit consume a borrow only where the read clears both its
floor and a materiality gate.  Two limits bound the estimator,
each one sentence.  A flat rate error is indistinguishable
from a borrow error at a single expiry --- both are carry --- so
splitting them needs an external rate curve, and
\eqref{eq:fwdborrowdef} inherits any rate bias one for one.  And on
American chains the early-exercise premium moves with the very carry
being solved for, so the borrow and the de-Americanization must close
jointly --- one more reason this chapter hands its output to
Chapter~7.

\subsection{The contract}
\label{sec:fwdcontract}

The chapter closes the way the earlier ones do, with its contract ---
three columns of decreasing strength.  The first holds by theorem.
The second is measured, on the frozen chain or on the stated boards,
and the chapter quoted every number.  The third is convention:
chosen and stated, because no data selects it.

Step back to the book's thesis, one last time for this chapter.
Validity was parity itself --- an arbitrage statement no model can
opt out of.  Information was distributed exactly as
\cref{prop:fwdols} said it would be: abundant in the line's level,
scarce in its slope, absent past the borrow's floor.  Convention was
everything the data could not select --- the rate band, the dividend
model, the declared treatment of what cannot be measured.  One
contamination ran through the chapter unresolved: the American bow of
\cref{fig:fwdline}(b), tolerated here, priced in
\cref{sec:fwdskew}, and entangled with the borrow just above.  The
next chapter removes it.
