\section{A prior on the rate, and the lever-arm identity}
\label{sec:fwdclamp}

Here is where the chapter stands.  The slope is the fragile parameter
(\cref{sec:fwdident}); some failures corrupt it without tripping any
alarm inside the chain (\cref{rem:fwdmask}).  On a slow or stale data
feed the corruption can go further than the staged examples: the
fitted discount drifts above one, which means a \emph{negative}
implied rate on an ordinary US equity chain.  The question this section answers is
what to do when the data's least reliable parameter contradicts
something we know from outside the data.

The answer is a prior --- prior knowledge, imposed as a hard
constraint.  A financing rate for a US equity has a physical range:
not below zero by percents, not above by tens.  Fix a band of
plausible rates (its endpoints are a disclosed convention, reviewed
like any other; the reference implementation ships a wide one) and
apply one rule:
\emph{if the fitted rate $\hat r$ falls inside the band, keep the
regression's answer untouched; if it falls outside, stop trusting the
slope} --- set the discount to the band's edge, the nearest value
prior knowledge allows.  Clean chains sit strictly inside the band
and pass bit for bit, like the trim before: the device only exists on
demonstrably bad inputs.

But rejecting the slope creates an obligation.  The forward estimate
$\hat F=\hat a/\hat D$ has the rejected slope in its denominator; if
$\hat D$ was untrustworthy, so is $\hat F$ computed from it.  The
forward must be re-derived from what is still trustworthy --- the
level --- and \emph{how} to do that well is a small piece of
mathematics worth isolating, because it explains a design choice that
looks arbitrary otherwise.

Solve \eqref{eq:fwdparity} for $F$ one strike at a time: on a
noiseless board, $F=K_i+\Pi_i/D$ for every $i$.  So given any
discount $D'$ --- possibly the clamped one, possibly wrong --- a
whole family of \emph{level estimators} is available: pick weights
$\mu_i\ge0$ summing to one ($\mu_i$ in its Chapter~3 role, a weight
per quote) and average,
\[
\hat F_\mu(D')=\sum_i\mu_i\Big(K_i+\frac{\Pi_i}{D'}\Big).
\]
The question is how much a wrong $D'$ hurts, and the answer is exact
and short.

\begin{proposition}[the lever arm of a level estimator]
\label{prop:fwdlever}
On a noiseless board obeying \eqref{eq:fwdparity},
\begin{equation}
\hat F_\mu(D')-F
=\Big(1-\frac{D}{D'}\Big)\big(\bar K_\mu-F\big),
\qquad
\bar K_\mu=\sum_i\mu_iK_i .
\label{eq:fwdlever}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the truth $\Pi_i=D(F-K_i)$ into one term of the average:
\[
K_i+\frac{\Pi_i}{D'}
=K_i+\frac{D}{D'}(F-K_i)
=K_i\Big(1-\frac{D}{D'}\Big)+\frac{D}{D'}\,F .
\]
Averaging with the weights $\mu_i$ replaces $K_i$ by $\bar K_\mu$:
\[
\hat F_\mu(D')
=\bar K_\mu\Big(1-\frac{D}{D'}\Big)+\frac{D}{D'}\,F
=F+\Big(1-\frac{D}{D'}\Big)\big(\bar K_\mu-F\big). \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

Read \eqref{eq:fwdlever} in words: the transmitted error is the
relative discount error times $\bar K_\mu-F$, the distance from the
estimator's center of weight to the forward.  That distance is a
\emph{lever arm}.  Where an estimator centers its strikes decides how
much a wrong discount can hurt it --- and the modeler chooses the
center.

\Cref{fig:fwdlever} measures the identity on a deliberately
asymmetric board (strikes from 85 to 140, so that averages are
pulled away from the money; constants in appendix
\ref{app:fwdprotocol}), imposing rate errors of up to two percent and
re-deriving the forward three ways.  In panel~(a), the three curves.
The steep one is the regression reading $\hat a/D'$.  It belongs to
the family too, in a degenerate way: the intercept $\hat a$ is the
fitted line's value extrapolated to strike \emph{zero} --- a strike
no board quotes --- so reading $\hat a/D'$ amounts to placing all
the weight at that phantom point, $\bar K_\mu=0$.  Its lever arm is
then $F$ itself, the worst available, and it transmits
\MacFwdLeverNaiveBpPerPct\,bp of forward error per percent of rate
error.  The middle curve is the uniform average over the board: its lever is
$\bar K-F$ --- mean strike \$\MacFwdLeverKbarDollars\ against forward
\$\MacFwdLeverFwdDollars, a
\$\MacFwdLeverUnifLeverDollars\ arm --- transmitting
\MacFwdLeverUnifBpPerPct\,bp per percent: better by an order of
magnitude, but only by the accident of where the board happens to
center.  The flat curve is the estimator
the reference implementation uses when its band bites: weights
concentrated \emph{at the money} by a Gaussian kernel in
log-moneyness (centered at spot --- the one anchor that needs no
estimate), whose center of weight lands at
\$\MacFwdLeverKbarKernDollars: a lever of
\$\MacFwdLeverKernLeverDollars, transmitting
\MacFwdLeverKernBpPerPct\,bp per percent.  The open circles confirm
the middle curve against \eqref{eq:fwdlever} evaluated directly ---
identity, not approximation.  Panel~(b) simply draws the three lever
arms on a log scale: \$\MacFwdLeverFwdDollars\ versus
\$\MacFwdLeverUnifLeverDollars\ versus
\$\MacFwdLeverKernLeverDollars.  The design lesson is compact: \emph{the clamp can
afford to be crude about the rate because the level estimator it
hands the problem to barely feels the rate at all.}  A last-resort
guard remains behind it --- if even the level answer lands absurdly
far from spot, the estimate falls back to spot itself and says so ---
and on such a chain no smile should be published at all.

One honest caveat closes the section.  The band is a prior, and a
prior can be wrong: a regime of genuinely negative dollar rates would
sit outside it, and the device would then clamp away information
rather than noise.  That is the standing trade of this chapter ---
stated once here, collected in the contract table of
\cref{sec:fwdborrow}.  What we now have is a defense in two layers,
each matched to a failure mode and each inert on clean data: the trim
for quotes that betray themselves, the band plus the short-lever
re-derivation for slopes that go absurd.  Between them sits the
failure neither catches --- coherent, plausible staleness --- bounded
by the level's robustness and nothing else.
