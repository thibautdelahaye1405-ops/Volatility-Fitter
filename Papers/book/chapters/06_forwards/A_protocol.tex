\section{Numerical protocol}
\label{app:fwdprotocol}

Every measured number in the chapter --- every fit result, scatter,
gap, share, or error --- comes from one deterministic generator and
enters the text as a generated macro.  Three kinds of numbers are
typed instead, deliberately: the constants of the constructions
themselves (the boards, schedules, and noise levels below, which
\emph{define} the experiments); arithmetic the reader is meant to
reproduce by hand (the two-strike solve of \cref{sec:fwdtwo}, whose
portfolio prices were placed exactly on the line with $D=0.98$ and
$F=101.50$, and the plug-in evaluations of
\cref{sec:fwdident,sec:fwdskew,sec:fwdborrow}); and stated
approximation constants such as $\phin(0)=0.399$.  Everything else
derives from the frozen snapshot or from the boards, in the order
the chapter uses them.

\emph{The real chain (\cref{fig:fwdline,fig:fwdskew}).}  The raw SPY
chain embedded in the book's frozen snapshot, December 2026 expiry.
A strike enters the parity fit when both its call and its put quote a
live (positive) bid; the observable is the difference of the two
mids, $\Pi=\mathsf{C}-\mathsf{P}$ with each mid the bid--ask
midpoint.  The naive fit is plain least squares over all surviving
pairs.  The \emph{resolved} forward quoted beside it is the one
stored with the snapshot --- the reference implementation's
resolution for that node, which de-Americanizes quotes before
regressing (Chapter~7) --- and the smile used in
\cref{fig:fwdskew} is the node's stored fit from the same snapshot.
The re-implied volatilities of \cref{fig:fwdskew}(b) solve the
one-dimensional Black inversion by bracketing to machine tolerance,
put side for $k<0$ and call side for $k>0$; in this data the
calendar and variance clocks coincide, as \cref{sec:fwdtwo} noted.

\emph{The running board
(\cref{fig:fwdident,fig:fwdtrim}).}  European and synthetic: spot
$S=100$, rate $r=4\%$, continuous dividend yield $q_d=1\%$, flat
volatility $22\%$, half a year to expiry; strikes from 70 to 130
every 2.5 (25 pairs); every mid priced by the Black formula and
rounded to the cent.  The identifiability audit adds independent
Gaussian noise of standard deviation two cents to every call and put
mid separately, refits \eqref{eq:fwdols}, and repeats for 2{,}000
trials from a fixed-seed generator --- the chapter's only use of
randomness; the short-dated variant repeats the experiment at
$t=0.05$.  (The noise rides on top of the board's cent rounding,
which adds $\approx0.3$ cents in quadrature; the predictions ignore
it, and the measured--predicted agreement shows how little it
matters.)  Predictions come from
\eqref{eq:fwdolssd}--\eqref{eq:fwddecomp} with the board's
$n$, $S_{KK}$, and truth.

\emph{Trim and masking (\cref{fig:fwdtrim},
\cref{rem:fwdmask}).}  The trim is the loop of \cref{sec:fwdtrim}
verbatim: robust scale $1.4826\times$ the median absolute residual,
floored at one basis point of spot; threshold four robust sigmas; at
most three rounds; never below three surviving pairs.  The staged
failure marks the strike-75 put mid \$1.20 high.  The masking
variant re-prices the five lowest strikes at a financing rate two
percent above the truth: their portfolio differences $\Pi$ are
recomputed exactly at the stale $(F',D')$ and rounded to the cent
directly (the difference, not the two mids --- immaterial at this
size, noted for exactness), and the identical trim runs.

\emph{The lever board (\cref{fig:fwdlever}).}  Same construction,
strikes from 85 to 140, prices kept exact (no rounding) so the
figure isolates the identity \eqref{eq:fwdlever}.  Imposed rate
errors span $\pm2\%$.  The kernel estimator weights each strike by a
Gaussian in $\log(K/S)$ of width $0.10$, centered at spot.

\emph{Dividends (\cref{fig:fwddivs}).}  The schedule pays \$0.65
each quarter, first ex-date 30 days out, horizon 2.5 years, at
$r=4\%$; the proportional variant converts each payment to the
fraction $0.65/100$ of the prevailing level; the flat yield is the
schedule's one-year equivalent.  Elasticities are measured by
recomputing each convention's forward under a one-basis-point spot
bump, against the closed form \eqref{eq:fwdelas}.

\emph{Borrow (\cref{fig:fwdborrow}).}  Closed forms only:
\eqref{eq:fwdbsens} at a flat $20\%$ volatility, and
\eqref{eq:fwdfloor} with $n=20$ paired strikes at quote noise of 10
and 50 basis points of spot.  The shaded hard-to-borrow range,
100--1{,}000\,bp per year, is an illustrative convention of the
figure, not an estimate.
