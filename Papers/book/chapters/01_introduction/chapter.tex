\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

\section{The predicament}
\label{sec:introproblem}

An equity-options desk publishes, every morning, an implied-volatility
surface for each name it trades: at every expiry a curve of
volatilities across strikes --- a \emph{smile} --- and, expiry by
expiry, the name's \emph{surface}.  Everything downstream consumes
these surfaces: client quotes, hedge ratios, risk aggregation,
scenario engines.  And they are consumed at every strike, including
the strikes nobody quoted today.

What the market supplies falls short of that in three ways.  It is
finite: a listed board quotes a ladder of strikes at a handful of
expiries, and nothing between or beyond.  It is noisy: a quote is a
bid and an ask, two prices with the truth somewhere in the spread;
some quotes are stale, and a few are simply wrong.  And it is
unevenly spread: on a liquid index board the ladder is dense near the
money, a few strikes further out the spreads widen, and on most of a
desk's universe --- a thin name that has not traded today, a long
tenor with four resting quotes --- the board is nearly silent.
Whole surfaces on one side; finitely many noisy prices on the other.

The published object is also more than a sheet of numbers.  A smile,
read through the Black formula, is a probability law for the
underlying at that expiry --- the next section says how --- so a
surface is a family of laws, and a desk's universe of surfaces is a
\emph{field of probability laws}: the object of this book's title.
The predicament, stated once: a market that quotes finitely many
noisy prices, and a desk that must nevertheless publish something as
ambitious as a field of probability laws --- a law for every
underlier, every expiry, every strike, every morning.  Everything in
this book serves that publication.

\section{A smile is a probability law}
\label{sec:introlaw}

Fix one expiry $T$ and one name, and let $\mu$ be the law of the
underlying price $S_T$ at that expiry (under the pricing measure; the
distinction does not matter for this section).  A call struck at $K$
pays $(S_T-K)^+$, so its forward price is the average of the payoff
over $\mu$,
\[
C(K)=\int (s-K)^{+}\,\mu(\dd s),
\]
and differentiating under the integral, twice, in the strike,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial C}{\partial K}=-\,\mu\bigl((K,\infty)\bigr),
\qquad
\frac{\partial^{2} C}{\partial K^{2}}=\text{the density of } S_T
\text{ at } K.
\label{eq:introBL}
\end{equation}
A complete call curve at one expiry therefore \emph{is} the law of
the underlying at that expiry, in price coordinates: the slope reads
off digital probabilities, the curvature reads off the density.  The
identity is due to \citet{BreedenLitzenberger1978}, and it has a
traded counterpart: a negative second difference of call prices is a
butterfly --- a nonnegative payoff --- with a negative price.  The
probability statement and the no-arbitrage statement are the same
statement.  Chapter~2 develops both in full.

Implied volatility enters as a change of coordinates, not as a model.
The market's convention is to state a call's price as the volatility
number that reproduces it through the Black formula
\citep{Black1976}; price and implied volatility are two coordinates
for one quantity, and the Black formula is the chart between them.
Nothing in this convention assumes the Black model describes the
underlying: the formula is a one-to-one relabeling, used because in
volatility units the prices of different strikes, expiries and names
become comparable at a glance.  The smile is the law of
\cref{eq:introBL} drawn in the market's preferred coordinates.

Across expiries the family of laws has one requirement of its own:
in total-variance units a later expiry must lie above an earlier one
--- \emph{calendar order}, derived in Chapter~2 --- or a calendar
spread of options acquires a negative price.  A field of laws is not
a stack of independent curves.

\section{Validity, information, convention}
\label{sec:introthesis}

The thesis that organizes every chapter is a separation.  Everything
a published surface asserts sorts into three bins, of decreasing
strength, and the book's work --- subject by subject, number by
number --- is keeping the bins separate.

\emph{Validity} is what probability alone demands.  A call curve must
be decreasing and convex (the density of \cref{eq:introBL} is
nonnegative); the law must carry unit mass and price the forward
correctly; across expiries the surface must keep calendar order.  A
published surface that fails one of these is not a cautious surface
or an aggressive one --- it is not a family of laws at all, and it
hands its consumers arbitrage: nonnegative payoffs at negative
prices.  Validity is therefore not a quality to trade off against
fit.  Part~I builds coordinates in which most of it holds
structurally, so that every curve a fit can produce is already a law.

\emph{Information} is what today's quotes determine, and how
precisely.  It is always finite, and it runs out closer than
intuition suggests: on the book's own data, a measurable share of a
variance-swap strike accrues beyond the last quoted strike
(Chapter~5); a morning thinned to its at-the-money band says nothing
at all about the wing (Chapter~10); and no snapshot, however dense,
determines how the smile responds when the spot moves (Chapter~9).
Where information ends is itself measurable, and the book measures it
repeatedly --- with error bars whose own correctness is tested.

\emph{Convention} is everything the published object needs beyond the
first two bins: the wing beyond the last quote, the clock that turns
calendar days into variance time, the smile's response to a spot
move, the weight of yesterday against a thin morning, the asserted
relations between names.  A convention is not a flaw; an unstated one
is.  The book's treatment of a convention is always the same: derive
what can be derived, bound what can be bounded, and state the
remainder as a named choice with its consequences measured.

Each content chapter closes by sorting its own subject into a
three-column table --- proved, measured, chosen --- and Part~III adds
the test that disciplines the middle column: a stated variance must
match the realized errors (the standardized-error test of
Chapter~10, $\mathrm{std}(\mathcal{Z})=1$), and the book applies it
to every construction that publishes uncertainty.

\section{The reader, and how the book reads}
\label{sec:introreader}

The reader assumed here is smart, motivated, and new to this subject:
calculus and probability at first-course level, and the
Black--Scholes formula seen once.  No prior exposure to volatility
modeling is assumed.  Each chapter assumes the earlier ones, read
once.

The writing follows from that assumption.  Every derivation a
first-course reader could not reproduce is written out --- completing
a square, inverting a $2\times2$ matrix, differentiating under an
integral.  The smallest example comes first: three quotes, a two-node
graph, one worked number the reader can check by hand.  Figures are
walked panel by panel in the text.  Terms of art are re-introduced
with a one-line reminder at first use in each chapter, so no chapter
must be read with a glossary open.

Notation is kept on a ledger: a symbol, once given a meaning, keeps
it for the whole book, and no symbol is redefined.  Standing
conventions: primes denote one-variable derivatives and $\partial$
partial ones; $t$ is calendar time and $\tau$ is \emph{variance
time}, equal to the calendar until Chapter~8 separates them;
\emph{precision} always means one over a variance.

The subject is the published surface, not derivative pricing at
large.  Stochastic-volatility and jump models, exotic payoffs, and
hedging beyond delta stay outside the book; where a dynamic
assumption is unavoidable (Chapter~9), it enters as a stated
transport rule, derived where it can be and named a convention where
it cannot.

\section{The reference implementation, and one morning of data}
\label{sec:introimpl}

Two standing facts about the book's examples.

First, every construction in this book runs.  Alongside the text
stands one code base --- called throughout \emph{the reference
implementation} --- in which each model, solver and test of the book
exists as working code, and from which every figure and every
empirical number in the text regenerates.  The text names it as an
actor for a specific reason.  Mathematics does not force every choice
a working system must make: a floor on an eigenvalue, a default
variance, a cap on an inflation multiple, a tie-break among equal
minima.  Such choices are conventions, and conventions live in
implementations, not in theorems.  When a chapter writes ``the
reference implementation floors the small eigenvalues,'' it is
stating such a choice --- a working default, named so that it can be
adopted, replaced, or tested.

Second, one market snapshot threads every example: the listed option
boards of two names --- the S\&P~500 ETF (SPY) and NVIDIA (NVDA) ---
captured in one session in August 2026, eight expiries each, with
the full chains and quotes embedded, then frozen.  Nothing in the
book refetches data, and every figure regenerates from that one
file.  One dataset, used throughout, lets the examples compound: the
December index smile fitted in Part~I is the smile whose
variance-swap strike Chapter~5 integrates, whose forward Chapter~6
re-derives from the quotes, whose transported image Chapter~9 moves,
and whose thinned morning Chapter~10 fits; the December NVDA smile
is the one Chapter~11 withholds from its solver and reconstructs
from the node's neighbours.  A number met twice can be checked
twice.

\section{The map}
\label{sec:intromap}

\paragraph{Part I --- Models.}
Four chapters build and probe the single-name surface: three ways to
complete finitely many quotes into a law, and one measurement of what
the completions say where no quote reaches.

Chapter~2 builds the law first.  The log-return is written as a
monotone transport of a logistic variable, and the logarithm of the
transport speed is expanded in polynomials of the percentile rank:
positive density and unit mass are then automatic, one scalar shift
imposes the martingale condition, and validity is structural rather
than checked.  The same representation yields prices, densities, the
moment strip and the wing slopes, an analytic calibration Jacobian,
and a complete calendar test.

Chapter~3 does what the industry does instead: it writes the smile
down directly, as a five-parameter formula for total variance (SVI),
restated in the trader's coordinates of level, skew and wings.  The
gain is legibility; the cost is that a formula for a curve is not
automatically a law, so validity must now be checked rather than
inherited.  The chapter maps exactly where the checks bind, adds a
superposition family that gives the body of a smile local detail
without touching its wings, and closes by fitting both families and
Chapter~2's model to the same frozen quotes.

Chapter~4 asks for one model that carries every expiry at once.
Dupire's local volatility supplies it: a field whose value is a
ratio of the two quantities the earlier chapters already police ---
the calendar increment above, the butterfly factor below.  Read as a
formula, the ratio differentiates noisy quotes twice and fails no
matter how good the data gets; read as a pricing model --- a
positive sheet on a grid, a stable marching scheme, exact
derivatives --- it becomes a well-posed fit like every other in the
book.

Chapter~5 probes the fitted curves where there are no quotes.  The
probe is one traded number, the fair strike of a variance swap: an
integral of the whole smile, a measurable share of which accrues
beyond the last quoted strike.  Models that agree to basis points at
every quote disagree on it.  Two model-free facts bound the
disagreement --- a ceiling on how steep any wing can be, and an
envelope, fanning out from the last quote, that no arbitrage-free
continuation can leave --- and everything inside the envelope is a
choice the modeler must make and state.

\paragraph{Part II --- The observation.}
Part~I treated the quotes as ready.  Part~II prepares them.

Chapter~6 infers the forward and the discount factor, which are
quoted nowhere.  Put--call parity makes the pair the two
coefficients of one straight line through the option quotes, so the
inference is a regression --- one that determines its level superbly
and its slope poorly, an asymmetry every safeguard in the chapter is
matched to.  Dividend conventions turn out to be risk decisions, and
the stakes are measured in the only units that matter here: a small
forward error, read back through the Black formula, imprints a fake
skew of tens of volatility basis points.

Chapter~7 measures and subtracts the right that listed equity
options carry and the European identities know nothing about: early
exercise.  Optimal-stopping theory maps where the right has value
before anything is computed; a binomial tree prices it; the removal
is one root find per quote, a controlled cancellation in which the
market's premium cancels against the model's.  Dividends enter as
dates, not rates.  The chapter closes Chapter~6's loop: the bow that
early exercise had left in the parity residuals is reproduced by the
tree and removed.

Chapter~8 separates variance time from the calendar.  A scheduled
event --- an earnings report --- packs several ordinary days'
variance into one calendar day, and the law at expiry knows only the
variance budget, not its schedule.  The day-weighted event clock
relabels time so that prices, validity and calendar order are
untouched while volatility readings rescale exactly; the famous
overnight earnings crush becomes a scheduled correction of a
reading, with no price moving.  The clock can even be read off the
board's own prices --- within measured limits the chapter is
explicit about.

\paragraph{Part III --- Dynamics and inference.}
The field so far is a photograph.  Part~III sets it in motion and
then confronts it with sparse mornings.

Chapter~9 takes the derivative the photograph does not contain: the
smile's response to a spot move.  Through today's surface pass many
admissible tomorrows, and no quoted price separates them --- yet the
true delta of every option depends on the choice.  The candidate
rules are organized by one dial, the skew-stickiness ratio
$\mathcal{R}$, and the one regime that derives its answer instead of
assuming it --- repricing under a frozen local-volatility field ---
gives $\mathcal{R}=2$ as a consequence, not a convention.  The
stakes, measured across the frozen board, are worth double-digit
delta points.

Chapter~10 is inference under weak information, one node at a time.
It measures where a sparse morning's likelihood is flat; lets
yesterday enter today's fit through a gate that is exactly zero
wherever the data identifies a coordinate; prices the
identifiability of any smile functional by a harmonic law; and, in
time, filters the smile's handles with a gain that is computed, not
set --- the ratio of two variance budgets, one built from the fit's
own information, one grown on the market's clock.  It ends with the
accounting identity that counts each quote once, and with the book's
acceptance test: the standardized error $\mathcal{Z}$ scatters with
standard deviation one exactly when the stated variances match the
realized errors.

Chapter~11 assembles everything into the object the predicament
demands: a universe of nodes, mostly dark, completed from the few
that are lit.  Each edge of the graph is a two-number contract ---
amplitude prices the implied move, trust prices the band --- and the
whole-universe solve is a single least-squares objective,
Chapter~10's update at universe scale, whose accounting holds line
by line.  A dark node's answer decomposes into baseline, systematic
and residual, and the closing exhibit reconstructs a withheld
December smile from its neighbours, to within a fraction of a
volatility point of quotes the solver never saw, under a band that
passes the standardized-error test.  Its final pages return to the
predicament of \cref{sec:introproblem} --- which is where we begin.
