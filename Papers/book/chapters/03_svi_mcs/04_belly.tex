\section{The belly escapes}
\label{sec:svibelly}

The word ``convex'' hides two different second derivatives.  The family
\eqref{eq:svi} has $w''>0$ by construction; a butterfly spread tests the
convexity of the option \emph{price} in strike, which is the sign of
$g_{\rm D}$, and $g_{\rm D}$ mixes $w$, $w'$, and $w''$.  In the wings the
two notions agree --- \cref{prop:gtail} showed the sign of $g_{\rm D}$
there is Lee's --- but in the interior they need not.  The floor condition
of \cref{tab:tiers} fences the vertex, the cap \eqref{eq:leecap} fences
the wings, and between them lies a region neither condition reaches.

That the gap is real, and not a defect of any particular fitting code, is
a classical example due to Vogt, reproduced as Example~3.1 by
\citet{GatheralJacquier2014}:
\[
(a_S,b_S,\rho_S,m_S,s_S)=(-0.0410,\ 0.1331,\ 0.3060,\ 0.3586,\ 0.4153).
\]
Its minimum total variance is $w^*=\MacSviVogtWmin$, so the floor is
clean.  Its larger wing slope is $\MacSviVogtLee$, an order of magnitude
under the cap, and by \eqref{eq:gtail} both tails of $g_{\rm D}$ settle
near $\MacSviVogtTail$ --- the slice is impeccable everywhere the two
inequalities look.  Yet $g_{\rm D}$ dips to $\MacSviVogtGmin$ near
$k=\MacSviVogtKdip$, squarely between the two clean tails: the implied
density is negative there, and the slice prices a butterfly below zero.
\Cref{fig:svivogt} shows both halves.  Strict convexity of $w$ did not
protect strike-convexity of the price.

Could the gap be closed by a better inequality?  In principle yes: the
exact butterfly-free domain of \eqref{eq:svi} has been characterized
completely by \citet{MartiniMingone2022}.  But the characterization is not
one short inequality --- it involves a parameter rescaling, root finding,
and a numerical minimization --- and it is specific to this five-parameter
family, with no analogue for the superposition family of \cref{sec:mcs}.
The reference implementation therefore adopts the same division of labor
for every family in the volatility chart, and it is worth stating as the
chapter's second convention.  During optimization, the floor and cap enter
as two hinge rows appended to the residual,
\begin{equation}
W\,\max\!\big(-(a_S+b_Ss_Sq_S),\,0\big),
\qquad
W\,\max\!\big(b_S(1+|\rho_S|)-\beta_{\max},\,0\big),
\label{eq:svipenalties}
\end{equation}
with a multiplier $W$ large enough to dominate a violation; both rows are
exactly zero on a slice satisfying the two inequalities, so a clean fit is
identical with or without them.  After optimization, the full condition is
checked where the inexpensive rows cannot reach: $g_{\rm D}$ is evaluated
from the model's own derivatives --- never by differencing prices --- on a
dense grid across the traded strike range, and the slice is accepted only
if the sampled minimum clears a small numerical tolerance.  This is the
same posture as the numerical certificates of \cref{sec:certificates}: an
analytic guarantee where one exists, and where it does not, a computed
check on the finished object, with its scope --- the sampled grid on the
traded range, not the whole line --- stated rather than implied.  The
minimum of $g_{\rm D}$ over that grid is the \emph{validity margin} that
the comparison of \cref{sec:comparison} reports for every fit of every
family.

The division matters in both directions.  The condition
$g_{\rm D}\ge0$ \emph{can} be pushed into the optimizer as one more soft
row --- a hinge on sampled values of $g_{\rm D}$ over a grid --- and the
superposition family of \cref{sec:mcs}, whose kernels can bend the
density where a hyperbola cannot, carries exactly such a row
(\cref{app:protocol}).  But a finite-weight hinge is a fence, not a
proof: it is traded against the data term, it vanishes only where the
sampled values already satisfy the inequality, and \cref{sec:comparison}
shows fits that fail the final check with the fence active.  Conversely,
trusting any of the fences alone would publish the Vogt slice.  Fence
cheaply, then check completely: the fences keep the optimizer in the
region where the check usually passes, and the check decides.
