\section{Superposition: capacity without touching the wings}
\label{sec:mcs}

\Cref{prop:convexww} left a gap: some smiles --- ahead of a binary event,
or in a name with bimodal positioning --- carry a central trough with a
shoulder on each side, and no globally convex slice can follow them.  The
design problem is sharper than ``add flexibility.''  The wings are the
solved part of the curve: Lee's bound caps them, the quoted wing strikes
pin them, and \cref{sec:svilee} showed the tail of the density reads them
alone.  What is missing is detail in the \emph{body}, and the correction
that supplies it must not move the wings at all.

The Multi-Core Sigmoid (MCS) solves this by superposition: a convex base
that owns the wings, plus localized corrections that are silent --- in
value, slope, \emph{and} curvature --- in both tails.  The construction
rests on one elementary mechanism, developed first.

\subsection{The base}

Work in the standardized moneyness
\begin{equation}
\zeta=\frac{k}{\sigma_{\rm ref}\sqrt\tau},
\label{eq:zetadef}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ is a reference volatility fixed before the fit
(the quoted volatility nearest the money), so $\zeta$ measures moneyness
in standard deviations and the parameters are roughly scale-free across
expiries.  Displacements along this axis are written $\bar\zeta$
(measured from a center fixed by context, so the ledger's rank $u$ stays
untouched).  The convexity primitive is the log-cosh
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_\kappa(\bar\zeta)=\frac{4}{\kappa^2}\log\cosh\frac{\kappa\bar\zeta}{2},
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_\kappa'(\bar\zeta)=\frac{2}{\kappa}\tanh\frac{\kappa\bar\zeta}{2},
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_\kappa''(\bar\zeta)=\operatorname{sech}^2\frac{\kappa\bar\zeta}{2},
\label{eq:acosh}
\end{equation}
a smooth, even, convex softening of the absolute value.  Two facts about
it carry the section.  Near the origin
$\mathcal{A}_\kappa(\bar\zeta)=\bar\zeta^2/2+O(\bar\zeta^4)$: a rounded
vertex.  Far out it is \emph{asymptotically affine},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_\kappa(\bar\zeta)=\frac{2}{\kappa}|\bar\zeta|
-\frac{4}{\kappa^2}\log2+o(1),
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
since $\log\cosh x=|x|-\log2+\log(1+e^{-2|x|})$.  The base models
annualized variance $V(\zeta)=w/\tau$ as
\begin{equation}
V_{\rm base}(\zeta)=V_0+V_1(\zeta-\zeta_0)
+V_2\,\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_i}(\zeta-\zeta_0),
\qquad
\kappa_i=\begin{cases}\kappa_P,&\zeta<\zeta_0,\\
\kappa_C,&\zeta\ge\zeta_0,\end{cases}
\label{eq:mcsbase}
\end{equation}
six parameters: level $V_0$, slope $V_1$, and convexity amplitude
$V_2\ge0$ at the center $\zeta_0$, with separate put and call
steepnesses.  Because $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$ and $\mathcal{A}_\kappa'$
vanish at the origin and $\mathcal{A}_\kappa''(0)=1$ whatever $\kappa$,
the curve is $C^2$ across $\zeta_0$ even though the steepness switches
there: the asymmetry lies in how fast each wing straightens, not in a
kink or a curvature jump.  By \eqref{eq:affine} the asymptotic slopes of
$V_{\rm base}$ are $V_1-2V_2/\kappa_P$ on the left and
$V_1+2V_2/\kappa_C$ on the right; converting through \eqref{eq:zetadef}
and $w=\tau V$, the total-variance wing slopes in $k$ are
\begin{equation}
\beta_P=\frac{\sqrt\tau}{\sigma_{\rm ref}}
\Big(\frac{2V_2}{\kappa_P}-V_1\Big),
\qquad
\beta_C=\frac{\sqrt\tau}{\sigma_{\rm ref}}
\Big(V_1+\frac{2V_2}{\kappa_C}\Big).
\label{eq:mcsbetak}
\end{equation}
These six numbers set the wings; the design goal is that nothing added
later moves them.

\subsection{A kernel that vanishes to second order}

The correction kernel is the normalized centered second difference of the
same primitive: with $\bar\zeta=\zeta-\zeta_r$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta_r,\ell_r,\kappa_r}(\zeta)
=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\bar\zeta-\ell_r)
-2\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\bar\zeta)
+\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\bar\zeta+\ell_r)}
{2\,\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)},
\label{eq:hatdef}
\end{equation}
centered at $\zeta_r$, of half-width $\ell_r$ and steepness $\kappa_r$,
normalized so that $\mathcal{B}(\zeta_r)=1$ (the numerator at
$\bar\zeta=0$ is $2\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)$ because $\mathcal{A}$
is even with $\mathcal{A}(0)=0$).  Its derivatives are the same second
difference of $\mathcal{A}'$ and $\mathcal{A}''$ over the same
normalizer.

\begin{lemma}[Zero wings]
\label{lem:zerowing}
$\mathcal{B}\to0$, $\mathcal{B}'\to0$, and $\mathcal{B}''\to0$ as
$\zeta\to\pm\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A centered second difference annihilates affine functions: for any
affine $L$,
$L(\bar\zeta-\ell)-2L(\bar\zeta)+L(\bar\zeta+\ell)=0$ identically.  By
\eqref{eq:affine}, $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$ is affine plus $o(1)$ in each
tail, so its second difference tends to zero there.  The same argument
applies to $\mathcal{A}'$, which tends to the constants $\pm2/\kappa$
(affine of zero slope), and to $\mathcal{A}''$, which decays.
\end{proof}

The lemma is the whole design, and it does not depend on the log-cosh:
\emph{any} asymptotically affine primitive yields a tail-silent kernel by
the same second difference.  The log-cosh is a convenience --- the model
and its Jacobian share one set of derivatives, the kernel has unit height
so its amplitude reads directly as a variance displacement, and the
half-width and steepness are separate dials where a Gaussian bump has
one.  At its center,
$\mathcal{B}''(\zeta_r)
=\{\operatorname{sech}^2(\kappa_r\ell_r/2)-1\}/\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)<0$,
so a positive multiple is locally concave --- a shoulder --- and a
negative multiple digs a notch.

\subsection{The model, and what the superposition preserves}

The MCS slice is the base plus $M$ signed kernels,
\begin{equation}
V_M(\zeta)=V_{\rm base}(\zeta)
+\sum_{r=1}^{M}\gamma_r\,\mathcal{B}_{\zeta_r,\ell_r,\kappa_r}(\zeta),
\qquad
w(k)=\tau\,V_M\!\Big(\frac{k}{\sigma_{\rm ref}\sqrt\tau}\Big),
\label{eq:mcsmodel}
\end{equation}
with $6+4M$ parameters.  By \cref{lem:zerowing}, every member of the
family has the same asymptotic wings as its base, whatever $M$:
expressiveness in the body and control of the tails are decoupled by
construction.  \Cref{fig:mcsmech} shows the mechanism end to end on a
target built to be globally admissible --- a hyperbolic total-variance
base with two Gaussian shoulders, asymptotically linear wings, and
$\min g_{\rm D}=\MacMcsTargetGmin$ verified from the analytic
derivatives on a dense grid to $|k|=12$, with the positive tail limit
\eqref{eq:gtail} taking over beyond.  The convex base alone ($M=0$)
misses the shoulders by $\MacMcsBaseMaxErrBp$ vol bp, and the residual it
leaves is structured: two localized features, not noise.  Two kernels
reduce the miss to $\MacMcsFullMaxErrBp$ vol bp, and the fitted slice
remains butterfly-free ($\min g_{\rm D}=\MacMcsFitGmin$ on the wide
diagnostic grid).

\label{fig:mcsmech}
\end{figure}

Two honest boundaries of the construction, each one sentence.
\emph{Wing-neutral is weaker than wing-admissible}: the kernels preserve
the base slopes \eqref{eq:mcsbetak}, but nothing in the family forces
those slopes into $[0,2]$ --- admissibility of the base is a separate
condition, checked like any other.  And \emph{the family is not
arbitrage-free by construction}: a signed kernel can drive variance
negative or break price convexity locally, so the dense-grid check of
\cref{sec:svibelly} decides for MCS exactly as it does for SVI, and the
parameters $(\gamma_r,\zeta_r,\ell_r,\kappa_r)$ are means rather than
objects --- two overlapping kernels can trade amplitude, so only the
fitted curve is comparable across fits.

\subsection{Capacity and its governance}
\label{sec:mcscapacity}

The number of cores $M$ is the family's capacity dial, and counting
degrees of freedom already bounds it: $6+4M$ parameters against $n_q$
quotes requires
\begin{equation}
M\;\le\;\Big\lfloor\frac{n_q-6}{4}\Big\rfloor
\label{eq:idcap}
\end{equation}
for the fit to be determined at all, the analogue for this family of the
order guard \eqref{eq:orderguard}.  But identification is the weaker
constraint.  A kernel is a local feature detector, and on a smile that
has no local features it will find quote noise instead: the fit error
against the quotes falls while the error against the underlying curve
rises, and the manufactured curvature appears exactly where
\cref{sec:svibelly} taught us to look --- in the sparsely quoted region,
as a negative dip of $g_{\rm D}$.  \Cref{sec:comparison} measures this on
the frozen snapshot: at $M=2$ on a liquid index chain the family fits the
mid quotes better than SVI on every node while failing the validity
check on most of them; at $M=0$ the margins are uniformly clean and the
fit quality is SVI's.  The capacity is real and so is its price.  The
reference implementation therefore treats $M$ as a governed dial ---
capped by \eqref{eq:idcap}, ridged in amplitude, and reserved for smiles
that are genuinely non-convex --- rather than a default.  The calibration
itself is layered to match the model's structure: the base is fitted
first, the kernels are then seeded on the largest remaining residuals
(one feature each), and a final joint refinement adjusts all $6+4M$
parameters together.
