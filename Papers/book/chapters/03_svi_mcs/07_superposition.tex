\section{Superposition: capacity without touching the wings}
\label{sec:mcs}

\Cref{prop:convexww} left a gap: some smiles --- ahead of a binary
event, or in a name whose holders are split between two scenarios ---
carry a central trough with a shoulder on each side, and no globally
convex slice can follow them.  We now fill the gap.  But the design
problem is sharper than ``add flexibility.''  The wings are the
\emph{solved} part of the curve: Lee's bound caps them, the outermost
quotes pin them, and \cref{sec:svilee} showed that the tail density
reads them alone.  What is missing is detail in the \emph{body}, and
whatever supplies it must not move the wings at all.

The Multi-Core Sigmoid (MCS) meets this specification by
superposition: a convex base curve that owns the wings, plus localized
corrections that are silent --- in value, in slope, \emph{and} in
curvature --- in both tails.  The whole construction rests on one
elementary mechanism, and we build it first.

\subsection{The base}

The family works in a standardized moneyness
\begin{equation}
\zeta=\frac{k}{\sigma_{\rm ref}\sqrt\tau},
\label{eq:zetadef}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\rm ref}$ is a reference volatility fixed before the fit
(the quoted volatility nearest the money).  The scaling makes $\zeta$
count moneyness in standard deviations, so parameters mean roughly the
same thing at every expiry: with $\sigma_{\rm ref}=20\%$ and
$\tau=0.25$, one $\zeta$-unit is $0.20\times0.5=0.10$ of
log-moneyness; at $\tau=1$ it is $0.20$.  Displacements along this
axis are written $\bar\zeta$: the base below measures its displacement
from its center $\zeta_0$, a kernel from its own center $\zeta_r$, and
$\bar\zeta$ always denotes the distance from the center at hand.  (The
symbol $\zeta$ is new to this chapter's ledger;
Chapter~\ref{ch:lqd}'s $z$ is the log-odds of rank, an unrelated
object.)

The convexity primitive of the family is the log-cosh:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_\kappa(\bar\zeta)=\frac{4}{\kappa^2}\log\cosh\frac{\kappa\bar\zeta}{2},
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_\kappa'(\bar\zeta)=\frac{2}{\kappa}\tanh\frac{\kappa\bar\zeta}{2},
\qquad
\mathcal{A}_\kappa''(\bar\zeta)=\operatorname{sech}^2\frac{\kappa\bar\zeta}{2},
\label{eq:acosh}
\end{equation}
a smooth, even, convex softening of the absolute value, with $\kappa$
setting how fast the softening straightens out.  Two facts about it
carry the whole section.  Near the origin, $\log\cosh$ of a small
argument is half its square, so
$\mathcal{A}_\kappa(\bar\zeta)=\bar\zeta^2/2+O(\bar\zeta^4)$: a rounded
vertex, of unit curvature whatever $\kappa$.  Far out it is
\emph{asymptotically affine} --- straight line plus a vanishing
remainder.  To see this, write
\[
\cosh x=\frac{e^{x}+e^{-x}}{2}=\frac{e^{|x|}}{2}\big(1+e^{-2|x|}\big)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log\cosh x=|x|-\log2+\log\!\big(1+e^{-2|x|}\big),
\]
and the last term dies exponentially; scaling by $4/\kappa^2$ at
argument $\kappa\bar\zeta/2$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_\kappa(\bar\zeta)=\frac{2}{\kappa}|\bar\zeta|
-\frac{4}{\kappa^2}\log2+o(1).
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}

The base models annualized variance $V(\zeta)=w/\tau$ as level plus
tilt plus one rounded hinge:
\begin{equation}
V_{\rm base}(\zeta)=V_0+V_1(\zeta-\zeta_0)
+V_2\,\mathcal{A}_{\kappa}(\zeta-\zeta_0),
\qquad
\kappa=\begin{cases}\kappa_P,&\zeta<\zeta_0,\\
\kappa_C,&\zeta\ge\zeta_0,\end{cases}
\label{eq:mcsbase}
\end{equation}
six parameters: level $V_0$, slope $V_1$, and convexity amplitude
$V_2\ge0$ at the center $\zeta_0$, with separate put-side and call-side
steepnesses $\kappa_P$, $\kappa_C$.  Switching the steepness at
$\zeta_0$ costs no smoothness: $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$ and
$\mathcal{A}_\kappa'$ both vanish at the origin, and
$\mathcal{A}_\kappa''(0)=1$ for \emph{every} $\kappa$, so value, slope,
and curvature all match across the switch --- the curve is $C^2$, and
the asymmetry lies only in how fast each side straightens.

The wings of the base follow from \eqref{eq:affine}.  As
$\zeta\to+\infty$ the hinge contributes slope $2V_2/\kappa_C$, so
$V_{\rm base}$ has asymptotic slope $V_1+2V_2/\kappa_C$; on the left,
$V_1-2V_2/\kappa_P$, typically negative --- the put wing rises as
$\zeta$ falls.  To convert to the total-variance slopes that Lee's
bound reads, two bookkeeping steps: the map
$w(k)=\tau V(k/(\sigma_{\rm ref}\sqrt\tau))$ contributes a chain-rule
factor $\tau/(\sigma_{\rm ref}\sqrt\tau)=\sqrt\tau/\sigma_{\rm ref}$;
and on the put side the convention of \eqref{eq:sviwings} measures the
slope in $|k|$, which flips the sign of the falling left slope:
\begin{equation}
\beta_P=\frac{\sqrt\tau}{\sigma_{\rm ref}}
\Big(\frac{2V_2}{\kappa_P}-V_1\Big),
\qquad
\beta_C=\frac{\sqrt\tau}{\sigma_{\rm ref}}
\Big(V_1+\frac{2V_2}{\kappa_C}\Big).
\label{eq:mcsbetak}
\end{equation}
These six numbers set the wings.  The design goal for everything added
next is that nothing moves them.

\subsection{A kernel that vanishes to second order}

Here is the mechanism.  A centered second difference --- value at the
left, minus twice the value at the center, plus value at the right ---
annihilates affine functions exactly: for any straight line
$L(\bar\zeta)=\text{const}+\text{slope}\cdot\bar\zeta$,
\[
L(\bar\zeta-\ell)-2L(\bar\zeta)+L(\bar\zeta+\ell)
=\big(-\ell\cdot\text{slope}\big)+\big(+\ell\cdot\text{slope}\big)=0
\qquad\text{identically.}
\]
Now take the second difference of a function that is \emph{almost}
affine far out --- such as $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$, by \eqref{eq:affine}.
Far out, the second difference sees an affine function plus an
exponentially small remainder, and returns the remainder alone: a bump
that dies in both tails.  Near the center, where $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$
is genuinely curved, the second difference is not zero --- that is the
bump itself.

The correction kernel is exactly this, normalized: with
$\bar\zeta=\zeta-\zeta_r$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}_{\zeta_r,\ell_r,\kappa_r}(\zeta)
=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\bar\zeta-\ell_r)
-2\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\bar\zeta)
+\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\bar\zeta+\ell_r)}
{2\,\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)},
\label{eq:hatdef}
\end{equation}
centered at $\zeta_r$, of half-width $\ell_r$ and steepness $\kappa_r$.
The normalizer makes the peak height exactly one --- at
$\bar\zeta=0$ the numerator is
$2\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)$, because $\mathcal{A}$ is even with
$\mathcal{A}(0)=0$ --- so a kernel's amplitude will read directly as a
variance displacement.  Its derivatives are the same second difference
applied to $\mathcal{A}'$ and $\mathcal{A}''$, over the same
normalizer.

\begin{lemma}[Zero wings]
\label{lem:zerowing}
$\mathcal{B}\to0$, $\mathcal{B}'\to0$, and $\mathcal{B}''\to0$ as
$\zeta\to\pm\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:affine}, $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$ is affine plus $o(1)$ in
each tail; a centered second difference annihilates the affine part
exactly (the computation displayed above), so what remains tends to
zero.  The same argument covers the derivatives:
$\mathcal{A}'_\kappa$ tends to the constants $\pm2/\kappa$ in the
tails --- affine of zero slope --- so its second difference also tends
to zero, and $\mathcal{A}''_\kappa$ itself decays.
\end{proof}

The lemma is the entire design, and notice that it does not depend on
the log-cosh: \emph{any} asymptotically affine primitive yields a
tail-silent kernel by the same second difference.  The log-cosh is a
convenience --- the model and its derivatives share one set of
formulas \eqref{eq:acosh}, the unit peak makes amplitudes readable,
and the half-width $\ell_r$ and steepness $\kappa_r$ are separate
dials where a Gaussian bump has only one width.  One more reading will
be used below: the kernel's curvature at its own center.  Apply the
second difference to $\mathcal{A}''$ from \eqref{eq:acosh} at
$\bar\zeta=0$, using that $\mathcal{A}''$ is even with
$\mathcal{A}''(0)=1$:
\[
\mathcal{B}''(\zeta_r)
=\frac{\mathcal{A}''_{\kappa_r}(-\ell_r)-2\mathcal{A}''_{\kappa_r}(0)
+\mathcal{A}''_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)}{2\,\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)}
=\frac{\operatorname{sech}^2(\kappa_r\ell_r/2)-1}
{\mathcal{A}_{\kappa_r}(\ell_r)}<0,
\]
negative because $\operatorname{sech}^2<1$ away from zero.  So a
\emph{positive} multiple of $\mathcal{B}$ is locally concave --- it
builds a shoulder --- and a negative multiple digs a notch.

\subsection{The model, and what the superposition preserves}

The MCS slice is the base plus $M$ signed kernels:
\begin{equation}
V_M(\zeta)=V_{\rm base}(\zeta)
+\sum_{r=1}^{M}\gamma_r\,\mathcal{B}_{\zeta_r,\ell_r,\kappa_r}(\zeta),
\qquad
w(k)=\tau\,V_M\!\Big(\frac{k}{\sigma_{\rm ref}\sqrt\tau}\Big),
\label{eq:mcsmodel}
\end{equation}
with $6+4M$ parameters ($6$ for the base; center, half-width,
steepness, and amplitude $\gamma_r$ per kernel).  By
\cref{lem:zerowing}, every member of the family has the same
asymptotic wings as its base, whatever $M$: expressiveness in the body
and control of the tails are decoupled by construction.

\Cref{fig:mcsmech} shows the mechanism end to end, on a synthetic
target built to be globally admissible --- a hyperbolic total-variance
base with two Gaussian shoulders, asymptotically linear wings, and
$\min g_{\rm D}=\MacMcsTargetGmin$ verified from analytic derivatives
on a dense grid out to $|k|=12$ (\cref{app:protocol} records the
construction and why a target must be certified this carefully).
Panel~(a) shows the primitive $\mathcal{A}_\kappa$ merging with its
affine asymptote \eqref{eq:affine}: within a couple of units the two
are indistinguishable, and only the rounded vertex tells them apart
--- this exponential merging is what the kernel will inherit as tail
silence.  Panel~(b) shows one kernel \eqref{eq:hatdef}: a unit-height
bump (blue) whose slope (orange) and curvature both die visibly in the
tails --- silent to all three orders, as \cref{lem:zerowing} proved.
Panel~(c) runs the fit on the W-shaped target (orange dots).  The
convex base alone ($M=0$) cannot reach the two shoulders ---
\cref{prop:convexww} forbids it --- and misses by
$\MacMcsBaseMaxErrBp$ vol bp, leaving a residual that is not noise but
two localized features.  Adding two kernels (centers dotted) seats
both shoulders and cuts the miss to $\MacMcsFullMaxErrBp$ vol bp,
while the fitted slice remains butterfly-free:
$\min g_{\rm D}=\MacMcsFitGmin$ on the wide diagnostic grid.

Two boundaries of the construction, each stated once.
\emph{Wing-neutral is weaker than wing-admissible}: the kernels
preserve the base's slopes \eqref{eq:mcsbetak}, but nothing in the
family forces those slopes under the cap --- the base is screened like
any other slice.  And \emph{the family is not arbitrage-free by
construction}: a signed kernel can drive variance negative or break
price convexity locally, so the dense-grid check of \cref{sec:svibelly}
decides for MCS exactly as it does for SVI --- and since two
overlapping kernels can trade amplitude without changing the curve,
the fitted parameters are means to an end, and only the fitted curve
is comparable across fits.

\subsection{Capacity and its governance}
\label{sec:mcscapacity}

The number of cores $M$ is the family's capacity dial.  Counting
degrees of freedom already bounds it: $6+4M$ parameters against $n_q$
quotes requires
\begin{equation}
M\;\le\;\Big\lfloor\frac{n_q-6}{4}\Big\rfloor
\label{eq:idcap}
\end{equation}
for the fit to be determined at all --- the analogue, for this family,
of the LQD order guard \eqref{eq:orderguard}.  A thin chain with
fifteen quotes, for instance, admits at most
$\lfloor(15-6)/4\rfloor=2$ cores.

But identification is the weaker constraint, and the stronger one is
worth stating in words.  A kernel is a local feature detector.  On a
smile that has genuine local features --- an event trough, a second
mode --- it finds them.  On a smile that has none, it finds quote noise
instead: the error against the quotes falls while the error against
the underlying smile rises, and the manufactured curvature shows up
exactly where \cref{sec:svibelly} taught us to look --- as a negative
dip of $g_{\rm D}$ in a sparsely quoted region, where no data resists
it.  \Cref{sec:comparison} measures this on the frozen snapshot: at
$M=2$ on liquid index chains, MCS fits the mid quotes better than SVI
on every node and fails the validity check on most of them; at $M=0$
the margins are uniformly clean and the fit quality is SVI's.  The
capacity is real, and so is its price.

The reference implementation therefore treats $M$ as a governed dial
--- capped by \eqref{eq:idcap}, ridged in amplitude, and reserved for
smiles that are genuinely non-convex --- rather than as a default.
The calibration is layered to match the model's structure: fit the
base first; seed kernels one at a time on the largest remaining
residual features; then refine all $6+4M$ parameters jointly
(\cref{app:protocol} records the settings).
