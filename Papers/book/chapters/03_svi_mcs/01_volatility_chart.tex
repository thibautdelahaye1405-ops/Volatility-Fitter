\section{Working in the volatility chart}
\label{sec:svichart}

Chapter~\ref{ch:lqd} built a smile by choosing a probability law and reading
prices off it.  Validity was settled before calibration began: every
admissible coordinate vector was a law, and the Durrleman function
$g_{\rm D}$ of \eqref{eq:durrleman} was an identity rather than a
constraint.  The cost was indirection.  The coordinates $(L,R,a_n)$ describe
a transport speed in rank, and none of them is a quantity a trading desk
quotes.

This chapter takes the opposite route, which is also the industry's.  Write
the total implied variance $w(k)$ down directly, as a formula in
log-moneyness, with parameters chosen for legibility.  The gain is
immediate: the curve is the object everyone looks at, evaluation is one
line, and the parameters can be tied to the vocabulary of the desk --- ATM
level, skew, wing weights.  The cost is exactly the one
\cref{sec:intro} identified when it introduced $g_{\rm D}$: a direct
volatility model moves through a space that contains invalid curves, so
validity must now be policed --- partly by inequalities the optimizer
respects, partly by checks it cannot avoid.  The purpose of this chapter is
to develop that trade honestly on the two families of the reference
implementation that live in the volatility chart: SVI with its jump-wing
reading, and the Multi-Core Sigmoid built by superposition.

The chart serves two audiences with opposite tastes.  An optimizer wants a
formula that evaluates in one pass, differentiates in closed form, and
behaves sensibly outside the quoted strikes.  A desk asks where ATM
volatility sits, how steeply the smile leaves the money, how heavy each
wing is, and where the curve bottoms.  The stochastic-volatility-inspired
(SVI) family of Gatheral \citep{Gatheral2004} answers the first request
with a tilted hyperbola,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;w(k)=a_S+b_S\Big\{\rho_S(k-m_S)+\sqrt{(k-m_S)^2+s_S^2}\Big\},\;}
\label{eq:svi}
\end{equation}
five parameters under the structural conditions $b_S>0$, $|\rho_S|<1$,
$s_S>0$.  The subscript $S$ keeps the book's ledger unambiguous: bare
$\rho$ is the logistic density of \cref{sec:model} and bare $m$ its
martingale shift.  The desk's request is answered by a second coordinate
system on the same curve, the jump-wing handles of
\cref{sec:svijw}.  The two are exact re-parameterizations of one another on
a domain that \cref{thm:jwimage} makes precise.

What ``valid'' means must now be graded, because different conditions are
supplied by different mechanisms.  Five graded statements recur throughout
the chapter, collected once in \cref{tab:tiers}.  The first is guaranteed
by the optimization chart; the next two are inequalities cheap enough to
enforce during calibration; the fourth is the full butterfly condition
$g_{\rm D}\ge0$ of \eqref{eq:durrleman}, which no inexpensive screen
implies --- \cref{sec:svibelly} exhibits the classical slice that passes
every screen and fails it; the fifth couples expiries and is the subject of
\cref{sec:calendar}, whose hinge applies to these families unchanged.

The chapter runs as follows.  \Cref{sec:svigeom} establishes the geometry
of the hyperbola \eqref{eq:svi} --- including the rigidity that will later
motivate superposition.  \Cref{sec:svilee} reads the wings through Lee's
moment bound and locates that bound inside $g_{\rm D}$; the boundary case
produces the first failure.  \Cref{sec:svibelly} shows that the interior
escapes every wing condition.  \Cref{sec:svijw} develops the jump-wing
coordinates and proves exactly where they invert.  \Cref{sec:svicalib}
turns to calibration: the measure the objective integrates against, and
the chart that makes the enforced inequalities structural.
\Cref{sec:mcs} constructs the Multi-Core Sigmoid by superposition of a
convex base and tail-silent kernels.  \Cref{sec:comparison} then puts LQD,
SVI, and MCS on the same frozen quotes under one protocol and lets each
family's contract show.
