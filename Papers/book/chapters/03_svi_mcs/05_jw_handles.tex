\section{Trader coordinates: the jump-wing handles}
\label{sec:svijw}

The raw quintuple $(a_S,b_S,\rho_S,m_S,s_S)$ is an excellent
computational coordinate system and a poor market language.  No single
coordinate is ``the level'' or ``the skew,'' and each of $b_S$,
$\rho_S$, $s_S$ moves a wing and the interior at once.  The jump-wing
(JW) coordinates of \citet{GatheralJacquier2014} re-read the same curve
through five quantities chosen to match the desk's questions.  (The
name is inherited, not descriptive: the coordinates arose from reading
wing behavior in stochastic-volatility models with jumps; nothing in
this section requires jumps.)  This
section defines them, shows how to read them without mixing up their
units, and then answers the harder question: given five quoted handles,
can the hyperbola be reconstructed --- and when it cannot, what
information was lost?

A useful way to remember the five handles: three of them describe the
\emph{interior} of the smile and two describe its \emph{tails}.

\begin{definition}[Jump-wing handles]
\label{def:jw}
At variance time $\tau$, with $w_0=w(0)$ the ATM total variance, the
jump-wing coordinates of a slice are
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{v_J=\frac{w_0}{\tau},\qquad
\psi_J=\big(\sqrt{w}\big)'(0),\qquad
\tilde v_J=\frac{\min_k w(k)}{\tau}}_{\text{interior}},
\qquad
\underbrace{p_J=\frac{\beta_L}{\sqrt{w_0}},\qquad
c_J=\frac{\beta_R}{\sqrt{w_0}}}_{\text{tails}}.
\label{eq:jwdef}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

In words: $v_J$ is the ATM variance per year, $\psi_J$ is the ATM slope
(of total volatility $\sqrt w$, not of $w$ --- this matters below),
$\tilde v_J$ is the smile's minimum variance per year, and $p_J$, $c_J$
are the put and call wing slopes, each divided by $\sqrt{w_0}$.

For the family \eqref{eq:svi} all five have closed forms.  Four are
immediate from \cref{prop:svigeom}; the ATM slope takes two lines.
Since $(\sqrt w)'=w'/(2\sqrt w)$, and at $k=0$ the shorthands
\eqref{eq:svishort} read $x_S=-m_S$ and $r_S=\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}$,
formula \eqref{eq:svideriv} gives
$w'(0)=b_S\big(\rho_S-m_S/\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}\big)$, hence
\begin{equation}
v_J=\frac{w_0}{\tau},\quad
\psi_J=\frac{b_S}{2\sqrt{w_0}}\Big(\rho_S-\frac{m_S}{\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}}\Big),\quad
p_J=\frac{b_S(1-\rho_S)}{\sqrt{w_0}},\quad
c_J=\frac{b_S(1+\rho_S)}{\sqrt{w_0}},\quad
\tilde v_J=\frac{w^*}{\tau},
\label{eq:rawtojw}
\end{equation}
with $w^*$ from \eqref{eq:svivertex}.

\subsection*{Reading the handles in their own units}

Each handle lives in its own units, and misreading them is the most
common JW error, so we spell the dictionary out.  Write
$I(k)=\sqrt{w(k)/\tau}$ for the implied-volatility curve.  Then
$I(0)=\sqrt{w_0/\tau}=\sqrt{v_J}$; differentiating,
$I'(0)=w'(0)/(2\sqrt{\tau w_0})=(\sqrt w)'(0)/\sqrt\tau=\psi_J/\sqrt\tau$;
and inverting the definitions of the wing handles,
$\beta_L=p_J\sqrt{w_0}=p_J\sqrt{v_J\tau}$ and likewise for $\beta_R$:
\begin{equation}
I(0)=\sqrt{v_J},\qquad
I'(0)=\frac{\psi_J}{\sqrt\tau},\qquad
\min_kI(k)=\sqrt{\tilde v_J},\qquad
\beta_L=p_J\sqrt{v_J\tau},\qquad
\beta_R=c_J\sqrt{v_J\tau}.
\label{eq:jwunits}
\end{equation}
So: $v_J$ and $\tilde v_J$ are variances, and what an
implied-volatility chart shows is their square roots.  $\psi_J$ is the
ATM slope of total \emph{volatility}, so the tangent drawn on the IV
chart has slope $\psi_J/\sqrt\tau$.  And $p_J$, $c_J$ are
\emph{normalized} total-variance wing slopes --- they are not slopes of
the IV curve at all.

\Cref{fig:svihandles} annotates a fitted slice in exactly these units.
The data is the SPY December 2026 expiry of the frozen snapshot ---
the fixed market capture, described in \cref{app:data}, from which
every empirical example in this book is drawn --- and this particular
(asset, expiry) pair, a \emph{node} in the book's vocabulary, will
recur through the chapter.  The fit misses the quotes by
$\MacSviSpyRmsBp$ vol bp on average (a \emph{vol bp} is one hundredth
of a volatility percentage point, the book's unit of fit error), and
carries $\sqrt{v_J}=\MacSviSpyAtmVolPct\%$, tangent slope
$\MacSviSpyTangent$, floor
$\sqrt{\tilde v_J}=\MacSviSpyMinVolPct\%$, and wing handles
$(p_J,c_J)=(\MacSviSpyP,\MacSviSpyC)$.  Panel~(a) is the
implied-volatility chart, where the three \emph{interior} handles are
read: the orange dots are the mid quotes, the blue curve is the SVI
fit, the two dotted horizontals mark the level $\sqrt{v_J}$ and the
floor $\sqrt{\tilde v_J}$, and the dashed line through the money is the
ATM tangent, of slope $\psi_J/\sqrt\tau$ --- negative, as an equity
skew requires.  Panel~(b) switches to the total-variance chart, the
only place the two \emph{tail} handles can be read honestly: the two
dashed rays are the asymptotes, of slopes $\beta_L=p_J\sqrt{v_J\tau}$
and $\beta_R=c_J\sqrt{v_J\tau}$.  Plotting all five handles on one IV
axis would give the wing handles units they do not have.

One caution before inverting.  The tail handles are \emph{normalized}
indicators: by \eqref{eq:jwunits}, the actual asymptotic slopes --- the
objects Lee's bound and the moment budget \eqref{eq:momentmap} read ---
carry a factor $\sqrt{v_J\tau}$ of the supposedly interior level.
Raise $v_J$ with $(p_J,c_J)$ held fixed and both actual wings steepen,
moving the inferred moments.  JW is a quoting convention adapted to the
desk, not a decomposition of the slice into independent interior and
tail degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Inverting the handles}

Reading \eqref{eq:rawtojw} off a fitted slice is mere evaluation.  The
interesting direction is the reverse: a desk quotes five handles ---
can we reconstruct the hyperbola?  We build the inverse in three steps
and watch where it can break.

\emph{Step 1: the tail handles fix the scale and the tilt.}  From
\eqref{eq:rawtojw}, $p_J\sqrt{w_0}=b_S(1-\rho_S)$ and
$c_J\sqrt{w_0}=b_S(1+\rho_S)$.  Adding and subtracting,
\begin{equation}
b_S=\frac{\sqrt{w_0}}{2}(p_J+c_J),\qquad
\rho_S=\frac{c_J-p_J}{c_J+p_J},\qquad w_0=v_J\tau .
\label{eq:invwings}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 2: the ATM slope fixes where the vertex sits.}  The
combination of $m_S$ and $s_S$ that appears in $\psi_J$ is the
\emph{normalized vertex displacement}
$\chi:=m_S/\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}\in(-1,1)$.  Solve \eqref{eq:rawtojw} for
it: $\psi_J=\tfrac{b_S}{2\sqrt{w_0}}(\rho_S-\chi)$ and
$b_S/\sqrt{w_0}=(p_J+c_J)/2$ give
\begin{equation}
\chi=\rho_S-\frac{4\psi_J}{p_J+c_J}.
\label{eq:chidef}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 3: the gap between level and floor sets the width.}
Evaluate \eqref{eq:svi} at the money and at the vertex:
\begin{equation}
w_0=a_S+b_S\Big(\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}-\rho_Sm_S\Big),
\qquad
w^*=a_S+b_S\,s_S\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}.
\label{eq:twoids}
\end{equation}
By the definition of $\chi$, $m_S=\chi s_S/\sqrt{1-\chi^2}$ and hence
$\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}=s_S/\sqrt{1-\chi^2}$.  Subtract the second identity
from the first: the unknown $a_S$ cancels, and
\[
w_0-w^*
=b_S\,s_S\Big(\frac{1-\rho_S\chi}{\sqrt{1-\chi^2}}
-\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}\Big)
=b_S\,s_S\,\mathcal{D},
\]
where we name the bracket
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}(\rho_S,\chi)
=\frac{1-\rho_S\chi}{\sqrt{1-\chi^2}}-\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}.
\label{eq:Ddef}
\end{equation}
Since $w_0-w^*=(v_J-\tilde v_J)\tau$, the width and the remaining two
parameters follow:
\begin{equation}
s_S=\frac{(v_J-\tilde v_J)\,\tau}{b_S\,\mathcal{D}},\qquad
m_S=\frac{\chi\,s_S}{\sqrt{1-\chi^2}},\qquad
a_S=\tilde v_J\tau-b_S\,s_S\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}.
\label{eq:jwinverse}
\end{equation}

The whole inverse therefore stands or falls with the sign of the
denominator $\mathcal{D}$, and that sign is a disguised
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.  Consider the two unit vectors
$(\rho_S,\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2})$ and $(\chi,\sqrt{1-\chi^2})$ --- two
points on the upper half of the unit circle.  Their dot product is at
most $1$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_S\chi+\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}\sqrt{1-\chi^2}\;\le\;1,
\quad\text{i.e.}\quad
\mathcal{D}\;\ge\;0,
\label{eq:Dpos}
\end{equation}
(multiply the definition \eqref{eq:Ddef} through by the positive
$\sqrt{1-\chi^2}$ to see the two statements are the same), with
equality exactly when the two vectors coincide --- that is, when
$\chi=\rho_S$, which by \eqref{eq:chidef} means $\psi_J=0$.  This
single inequality gives both the domain of the coordinate system and
its one singularity.

\begin{theorem}[The image of the jump-wing map]
\label{thm:jwimage}
Fix $\tau>0$ and restrict \eqref{eq:svi} to $b_S>0$, $|\rho_S|<1$,
$s_S>0$, $w_0>0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A JW point has a unique inverse, given by
\eqref{eq:invwings}--\eqref{eq:jwinverse}, exactly on
\begin{equation}
v_J>0,\qquad p_J>0,\ c_J>0,\qquad
-\frac{p_J}{2}<\psi_J<\frac{c_J}{2},\qquad
\psi_J\ne0,\qquad \tilde v_J<v_J .
\label{eq:regdom}
\end{equation}
\item The image also contains the \emph{stratum} $\psi_J=0$,
$\tilde v_J=v_J$ (with $v_J,p_J,c_J>0$) --- a lower-dimensional sheet
of handle points --- every point of which is attained by infinitely
many slices.  There are no other image points.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $p_J,c_J>0$ then \eqref{eq:invwings} gives $b_S>0$ and
$\rho_S\in(-1,1)$; conversely every admissible slice has $p_J,c_J>0$
because $b_S>0$ and $|\rho_S|<1$ make both $\beta_L,\beta_R>0$.

Next, the band on $\psi_J$ is exactly the condition $|\chi|<1$, which
keeps $m_S$ finite in \eqref{eq:jwinverse}.  To see the equivalence,
note from \eqref{eq:invwings} that
\[
\rho_S-1=\frac{(c_J-p_J)-(c_J+p_J)}{c_J+p_J}=\frac{-2p_J}{p_J+c_J},
\qquad
\rho_S+1=\frac{2c_J}{p_J+c_J},
\]
so, by \eqref{eq:chidef},
\begin{align*}
|\chi|<1
&\iff \rho_S-1<\frac{4\psi_J}{p_J+c_J}<\rho_S+1\\
&\iff -\frac{2p_J}{p_J+c_J}<\frac{4\psi_J}{p_J+c_J}<\frac{2c_J}{p_J+c_J}
\iff -\frac{p_J}{2}<\psi_J<\frac{c_J}{2}.
\end{align*}
By \eqref{eq:Dpos}, $\mathcal{D}>0$ exactly when $\chi\ne\rho_S$,
i.e.\ $\psi_J\ne0$; and then $s_S>0$ in \eqref{eq:jwinverse} requires
$v_J>\tilde v_J$.  So on the domain \eqref{eq:regdom} the three steps
produce a unique admissible slice, and each failure of \eqref{eq:regdom}
breaks a requirement: $p_J\le0$ or $c_J\le0$ forces $b_S\le0$ or
$|\rho_S|\ge1$; $\psi_J$ outside the band sends $|\chi|\ge1$;
$\psi_J=0$ annihilates $\mathcal{D}$; $\tilde v_J\ge v_J$ makes
$s_S\le0$.

For the stratum: $\psi_J=0$ gives $\chi=\rho_S$, i.e.\
$m_S/\sqrt{m_S^2+s_S^2}=\rho_S$, which solves to
$m_S=s_S\rho_S/\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}$; substituting into
\eqref{eq:svivertex},
\[
k^*=m_S-\frac{s_S\rho_S}{q_S}
=\frac{s_S\rho_S}{\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}}-\frac{s_S\rho_S}{\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}}
=0
\]
--- the smile bottoms exactly at the money --- so the floor is
attained there and $\tilde v_J=v_J$ automatically.  Conversely, fix $(v_J,p_J,c_J)$ on the
stratum.  Then \eqref{eq:invwings} sets $(b_S,\rho_S)$, and for
\emph{every} width $s_S>0$, the slice with
$m_S=\rho_S s_S/\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}$ and
$a_S=v_J\tau-b_Ss_S\sqrt{1-\rho_S^2}$ reproduces the same five handles:
one handle set, a one-parameter family of slices.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The blind spot is the interior curvature]
\label{rem:stratum}
The free width on the stratum is not an abstract degeneracy; it is a
specific piece of missing information.  On the stratum the vertex sits
at the money, the handles pin the level and both tails --- and what
they omit is exactly how sharply the curve turns through its bottom.
By \eqref{eq:svivertex} the vertex curvature is
$\kappa^*=b_Sq_S^3/s_S$, which runs through every positive value as the
free width $s_S$ does.

Nor is the failure confined to the stratum itself; it is felt nearby.
Differentiate \eqref{eq:Ddef} in $\chi$ (quotient rule, then simplify
the numerator):
\[
\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}}{\partial\chi}
=\frac{-\rho_S(1-\chi^2)+\chi(1-\rho_S\chi)}{(1-\chi^2)^{3/2}}
=\frac{\chi-\rho_S}{(1-\chi^2)^{3/2}},
\]
which vanishes at $\chi=\rho_S$: the denominator is flat there, not
just zero.  Differentiate once more by the product rule,
\[
\frac{\partial^2\mathcal{D}}{\partial\chi^2}
=(1-\chi^2)^{-3/2}+3\chi\,(\chi-\rho_S)\,(1-\chi^2)^{-5/2},
\]
and evaluate at $\chi=\rho_S$, where the second term drops:
$\partial^2\mathcal{D}/\partial\chi^2=(1-\rho_S^2)^{-3/2}$.  So by
Taylor expansion, and then substituting
$\chi-\rho_S=-4\psi_J/(p_J+c_J)$ from \eqref{eq:chidef},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}
\sim\frac{(\chi-\rho_S)^2}{2\,(1-\rho_S^2)^{3/2}}
=\frac{8\,\psi_J^2}{(p_J+c_J)^2(1-\rho_S^2)^{3/2}},
\qquad\psi_J\to0.
\label{eq:Dasym}
\end{equation}
The denominator closes \emph{quadratically} in $\psi_J$.  Three
consequences, in increasing order of practical pain: near the stratum,
a finite width forces the gap $v_J-\tilde v_J$ to be of order
$\psi_J^2$, so the two handles carry nearly the same number; quote
noise in the handles is amplified by $1/\psi_J^2$ in the recovered
width; and a direct evaluation of \eqref{eq:Ddef} subtracts two nearly
equal numbers, losing precision exactly where precision is scarce.  An
implementation can remove that last cancellation by evaluating an
algebraically identical form --- but no rearrangement can restore a
curvature the handles never carried.  And the stratum is not exotic:
ATM sitting at the smile's minimum is where a desk would expect the
coordinates to be simplest, and fitted index slices pass close to it.
\end{remark}

\Cref{fig:svistratum} draws the two halves of the remark.  In
panel~(a), the denominator $\mathcal{D}$ is plotted against $\psi_J$ at
fixed $(p_J,c_J)$ (blue): it closes at $\psi_J=0$, and the dashed curve
--- the quadratic asymptote \eqref{eq:Dasym} --- hugs it near the
origin, confirming that the closing is quadratic, not linear.  In
panel~(b), three slices share one handle quintuple
$(v_J,0,p_J,c_J,v_J)$ on the stratum: same level, same asymptotes,
visibly different turns through the bottom, one per width $s_S$.  The
script verifies that the three slices agree on all five handles to
$\MacSviStratumSpread$ --- machine precision --- so nothing about the
handles distinguishes curves the eye tells apart at a glance.

We close the section with a practical dividend.  The validity screens
of \cref{tab:tiers} translate directly into handle vocabulary, so a
quoted handle set can be sanity-checked before any conversion: the
floor condition is $\tilde v_J\ge0$; the domain condition is
$-p_J/2<\psi_J<c_J/2$; and since
$\max(\beta_L,\beta_R)=\max(p_J,c_J)\sqrt{w_0}$ by \eqref{eq:jwunits},
the wing cap \eqref{eq:leecap} reads
\begin{equation}
\max(p_J,c_J)\,\sqrt{v_J\tau}\;\le\;\beta_{\max}.
\label{eq:jwcap}
\end{equation}
None of the three certifies the interior, for the reason
\cref{sec:svibelly} established: the belly answers to no cheap
inequality, in any coordinate system.
