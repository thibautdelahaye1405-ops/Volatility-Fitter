\section{Three families on the same quotes}
\label{sec:comparison}

The chapter closes by putting the three families side by side on the same
data: the frozen snapshot of \cref{app:data}, all \MacCmpNodes\ nodes,
under one protocol.  Each family is calibrated by the reference
implementation to the same mid quotes with equal weights --- LQD at order
16 under the guard \eqref{eq:orderguard} in its logistic chart, SVI in
the structural chart of \cref{sec:svistructural}, and MCS at its capacity
$M=2$ under \eqref{eq:idcap} --- and each fit reports the same three
quantities: the rms distance to the quotes, the validity margin
$\min g_{\rm D}$ over a dense grid on the traded range, and its wing
slopes.  Every Durrleman factor is evaluated from the family's own
analytic structure; \cref{app:protocol} records the details.  The point
of the exercise is not a ranking.  It is to watch each family's contract
--- what it guarantees, what it must have checked --- show up in numbers.

\subsection{The December node and the SPY gallery}

\Cref{fig:cmpnode} shows the SPY December 2026 slice.  The three fitted
curves are indistinguishable at chart scale; the information is in the
residual panel.  LQD tracks the mid quotes at $\MacCmpSpyLqdRms$ vol bp
rms with an unstructured residual.  SVI carries $\MacCmpSpySviRms$ vol bp
with the long-wavelength wave of a five-parameter curve absorbing a
seventeen-strike skew --- the visible cost of parsimony.  MCS at $M=2$
sits between, $\MacCmpSpyMcsRms$ vol bp, its two kernels spent on the
largest features of the SVI-shaped residual.  All three agree on the
wings to within a few hundredths of slope
($\beta_R=\MacCmpSpyLqdBetaR$, $\MacCmpSpySviBetaR$, and
$\MacCmpSpyMcsBetaR$ respectively), which is the pattern \cref{sec:tails}
led us to expect: no finite strip identifies an asymptotic slope by
itself, and three different conventions continue similar evidence three
different ways.

\Cref{fig:cmpgallery} extends the reading to the eight SPY expiries, and
its second panel is the one to study.  On fit quality the ordering is
stable: LQD, then MCS, then SVI, with the gap widening at the long end
where the skew's shape departs furthest from a hyperbola.  On the
validity margin the picture inverts.  LQD's margin is positive on every
node --- \cref{prop:noarb} made that structural, and the panel is simply
the numerical trace of a theorem.  SVI's margin is positive on every
node as well, not by theorem but by rigidity: five parameters fitted to
a liquid convex smile have no capacity left to bend the density
negative.  MCS at $M=2$ fails the check on most of the SPY expiries ---
its own sampled hinge on $g_{
m D}$ notwithstanding, the concrete form
of \cref{sec:svibelly}'s warning that a finite-weight fence bends the
optimum without bounding it.  Its kernels, having no genuine local features to explain on a liquid index
smile, chase quote noise, and the manufactured curvature surfaces as a
negative $g_{\rm D}$ dip --- typically in the thinly quoted region where
no data resists it.  Across all \MacCmpNodes\ nodes the medians read:

\subsection{Expressiveness measured on a legal target}

The gallery shows what excess capacity does where it is not needed.  The
complementary question --- what rigidity costs where capacity \emph{is}
needed --- must be asked against a target that an arbitrage-aware family
is allowed to reproduce.  A two-lognormal mixture supplies one: weights
$0.65$ and $0.35$, component volatilities $14\%$ and $42\%$, second
component forward $0.85$ and the first fixed by the martingale identity
($f_1=1.081$), quarter year to expiry.  Its law is genuine by construction, and the exact
density gives $\min g_{\rm D}=\MacCmpMixTargetGmin$ on the quoted range
--- bimodal, but strictly admissible.  \Cref{fig:cmpmixture} fits the
three families to its smile.  LQD follows the two modes at
$\MacCmpMixLqdRms$ vol bp with margin $\MacCmpMixLqdGmin$.  SVI misses by
$\MacCmpMixSviRms$ vol bp --- \cref{prop:convexww} in numbers, one belly
against two modes --- while remaining perfectly valid
($\min g_{\rm D}=\MacCmpMixSviGmin$): the failure is expressiveness, not
arbitrage.  MCS halves SVI's miss to $\MacCmpMixMcsRms$ vol bp but
overdrives its kernels into a small violation
($\MacCmpMixMcsGmin$): on this target its capacity is necessary yet
still needs its checks.  No family wins all three columns, which is the
result.

The comparison, the mixture, and the gallery say one thing three ways,
and it is the chapter's summary.  Working in the volatility chart does
not remove the constraint that a smile be a probability law; it moves
the constraint from the coordinates into the process --- inequalities
the chart can absorb, a check the chart cannot, and capacity that must
be governed because nothing structural governs it.  What the chart buys
in exchange is legibility: coordinates a desk can quote, wings that are
two readable numbers, corrections that are local by construction.
Chapter~\ref{ch:lqd} and this one are therefore not competitors but the
two poles of one design axis --- validity built into the coordinates
versus coordinates built for the reader --- and the choice between them
allocates difficulty; it does not eliminate it.  The next chapter moves
to a third position on the same axis: local volatility, where the
modeled object is neither a law nor a curve but the field consistent
with all of them at once.
