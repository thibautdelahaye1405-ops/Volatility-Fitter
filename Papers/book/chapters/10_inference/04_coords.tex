\section{Choosing coordinates: shape, not level}
\label{sec:infcoords}

The gate acts coordinate by coordinate.  That makes the choice of
coordinates load-bearing: gating can only keep yesterday out of what
today has measured if ``measured'' and ``unmeasured'' land in
\emph{different} coordinates.  This section proves that the trader's
baskets achieve exactly that separation, and then watches the proof
work in a controlled overnight experiment.

\subsection{What a penalty can damp}

Consider one basket $O=\sum_a\omega_a\sigma(k_a)$ over $L$ legs,
and compare two ways of persisting the same yesterday values: one
\emph{basket row},
$\sqrt{\lambda}\,\bigl(\sum_a\omega_a\sigma_a-\sum_a\omega_a
\sigma_a^{\mathrm{prior}}\bigr)$, against $L$ \emph{per-leg rows},
$\sqrt{\lambda_a}\,(\sigma_a-\sigma_a^{\mathrm{prior}})$ --- here
$\sigma_a$ stands for the fitted volatility at leg $a$, the
quantities the rows actually constrain.

\begin{proposition}[Baskets annihilate the level direction]
\label{prop:infnull}
The basket row is unchanged by every joint move
$\delta\in\R^L$ of the leg volatilities with
$\sum_a\omega_a\delta_a=0$ --- an $(L-1)$-dimensional family of
moves.  For the risk reversal ($\omega=(+1,-1)$) and the butterfly
($\omega=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-1)$) the coefficients sum to zero, so
this family contains the \emph{uniform lift} $\delta=(1,\dots,1)$:
adding the same amount to every leg leaves both rows exactly
unchanged.  The per-leg rows, by contrast, are unchanged only by
$\delta=0$; under a uniform lift of size $t$ their summed squared
cost grows as $t^2\sum_a\lambda_a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The basket row depends on the leg volatilities only through the
single number $\sum_a\omega_a\sigma_a$; any move $\delta$ with
$\sum_a\omega_a\delta_a=0$ leaves that number, hence the row, fixed.
The condition is one linear equation on $\delta\in\R^L$, so its
solutions form an $(L-1)$-dimensional space.  For RR,
$\sum_a\omega_a=1-1=0$; for BF,
$\tfrac12+\tfrac12-1=0$; in both cases $\delta=(1,\dots,1)$
satisfies the condition.  For the per-leg rows, the uniform lift
changes row $a$ by $\sqrt{\lambda_a}\,t$, so the summed squared cost
is $t^2\sum_a\lambda_a$, zero only at $t=0$.
\end{proof}

Read as market language: a risk reversal and a butterfly are
statements about \emph{shape} --- they are blind, by construction, to
the overall volatility level.  Persisting yesterday's shape through
baskets therefore exerts \emph{no} force against a market-wide level
move, while persisting yesterday's wing \emph{prices} strike by
strike fights such a move with weight equal to the full budget.  The
same information, expressed in different coordinates, either
respects a level jump or damps it.

\subsection{The overnight experiment}

\Cref{fig:infjump} runs the proposition as an experiment
(constants in Appendix~\ref{app:infprotocol}).  Yesterday's smile is
a smooth curve with a genuine wing --- skew and curvature both
nonzero.  Overnight the market jumps: today's true smile is
yesterday's \emph{lifted bodily by \MacPriorJumpPts\ volatility
points}, shape unchanged.  Today's quotes cover only the
at-the-money band, fifteen mids at stated noise of 30~bp.  The same
flexible spline family of \cref{sec:infflat} is fitted three ways:
with no prior at all; with gated \emph{shape baskets}; and with
\emph{absolute anchors} --- yesterday's wing volatilities imposed
directly at four wing strikes, the seemingly natural ``hold the
wings at yesterday'' rule.  The basket prior is built exactly by the
rules of the last two sections, with no hand in it: five candidate
rows (the level, and a risk reversal and a butterfly at each of the
leg pairs $\pm0.20$ and $\pm0.30$), each priced by the harmonic law,
each gated, and a fixed budget split over the open gates in
proportion to their values.

Walk panel~(a).  The orange dots are the morning; the dashed ink
curve is today's truth, the dotted grey curve yesterday's smile,
four points below it everywhere.  The data-only fit (orange curve)
matches the band and then extrapolates by its smoothness rule alone:
straight, missing the true wing's own curvature by
\MacPriorJumpDataErrDeep\ volatility points at $k=-0.30$ --- the
flapping of \cref{sec:infflat}, now with a truth to be wrong
against.  The violet curve is the anchor fit: it matches the band
too (the anchors sit outside it), but its wing bends down to
yesterday's \emph{absolute} values.  The blue curve is the basket
fit: it rides today's level --- which it learned from the band ---
and rebuilds the wing as ``today's level plus yesterday's shape,''
landing on the dashed truth.

Panel~(b) replots the three fits as errors against today's truth,
which makes the mechanism quantitative.  The blue basket curve hugs
zero across the entire drawn range,
\MacPriorJumpBasketErrDeep\ points off at the deep readout.  The
violet anchor curve descends, in the wings, toward the dotted line
at $-\MacPriorJumpPts$ points --- yesterday's level exactly: the
anchors have reconstructed \emph{yesterday's} wing under
\emph{today's} market, \MacPriorJumpAnchorErrDeep\ points wrong.
That is \cref{prop:infnull} in the flesh: absolute price rows
penalize the uniform lift, so they spend their budget pulling the
smile back down; basket rows are invariant under the lift, so their
entire budget goes into shape.

Two computed details close the case.  First, no damping: the basket
fit's at-the-money level agrees with the data-only fit's to
\MacPriorJumpAtmGapBp\ vol bp --- a fifteenth of the
\MacPriorJumpPts-hundred-bp jump.  Even that residue is indirect:
by \cref{prop:infnull} the basket rows exert exactly zero force
along the lift, but they do repaint the wings, and the smoothness
rule leaks a little of the repainting back into the band.  Second,
no hand-tuning: all five gates were computed, not set.  The
at-the-money support on this morning is
$\mathcal{Q}(0)=\MacPriorJumpSuppAtm$, far past the requirement, so
the level row's gate is $\operatorname{gate}=\MacPriorJumpGateAtm$
--- the prior contains \emph{no} row that could contest the level
even in principle (\cref{prop:infnodamp}).  The moderate-wing risk
reversal's gate stands open at \MacPriorJumpGateRr\ --- while the
moderate-wing \emph{butterfly's} is \MacPriorJumpGateBfMod: on the
same legs, its factor-of-four cheaper information price
(\cref{prop:infharmonic}) already clears the requirement, so the
gate drops it.  The deep-wing pair, dark to every measure, enters
essentially fully open (\MacPriorJumpGateRrDeep\ and
\MacPriorJumpGateBfDeep).  The budget concentrated itself on
exactly the three coordinates this morning left dark.

\subsection{The division of labour}

The experiment also shows why absolute anchors survive at all.  The
basket fit reconstructed the wing because the wing's \emph{shape
relative to the band} was yesterday's to give and the legs were
reachable.  But a basket needs its money-side reference: beyond the
last leg pair, in the truly deep tail, shape baskets run out --- and
\cref{sec:infblind}'s kernel bleed makes their gates unreliable
just past the band edge besides.  There, and only there, absolute
anchors are the tool, and they are placed by a different test than
the gate's.  Instead of radiated support, each candidate anchor
strike is scored by its \emph{coverage deficit}: compare the quote
mass actually present in that strike's own neighbourhood against
the mass a desired even coverage of the smile would put there, and
give the anchor weight only in proportion to the shortfall.  A
dense band a few strikes away contributes nothing to a wing
anchor's neighbourhood, so this test --- unlike the kernel sum of
\cref{eq:infsupport} --- cannot be talked out of a deficit by
nearby liquidity; where quotes already meet the desired coverage
the deficit, and the anchor, is zero.  The anchors then hold the
deep tail at yesterday's transported prices.  Their known cost is
exactly what panel~(b) displays --- across a level jump the deep
tail is pulled toward yesterday's absolute prices --- and the
division of labour confines that cost to strikes where no
alternative information exists at all.  Shape on baskets as far as
legs reach; absolute anchors only beyond; the level always to the
market.

Half of the chapter's program is now complete, and it is worth
saying in one sentence what has been built: \emph{along each
coordinate, the published smile is today's data where today's data
speaks, and yesterday's transported value where it does not, with
``speaks'' priced per functional and the handover proved not to
touch the identified coordinates}.  What this half cannot handle is
a morning whose quotes are present but sick --- dense, well inside
every gate's requirement, and internally contradictory.  For that,
the prior is the wrong instrument entirely, and time is the right
one.
