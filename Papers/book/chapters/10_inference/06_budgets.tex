\section{Two honest budgets}
\label{sec:infbudgets}

The update was one boxed display; the work is the two variances it
consumes.  This section builds each from first principles --- the
observation's from the fit itself, the prediction's from the clock
--- because any dishonesty in either budget becomes, through
\cref{eq:infupdate}, a wrong gain with nobody's hand on it.

\subsection{The observation budget: what did the market pin down?}

The observation is not a quote; it is a number read off a
\emph{fit}.  Its variance must answer: given where today's quotes
sit and how noisy each is, how precisely is this handle determined?

Warm up with one strike.  A single quote at the money, with stated
noise $\varepsilon$ (its bid--ask half-spread, read in volatility),
measures the level with variance $\varepsilon^2$; $n$ such quotes,
independently noisy, measure it with variance $\varepsilon^2/n$.
Precision is quote count over noise squared --- support again, now
in proper units.

The general case keeps exactly this structure.  Divide each quote
equation by its own stated noise $\varepsilon_i$, so that every row
of the least-squares stack is measured in units of its own noise
(``whitened,'' in the standard jargon).  Near the optimum the fit is
locally linear in its parameters $\theta$, and the whitened stack
carries an \emph{information matrix} --- the natural generalization
of ``count over noise squared'':
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_\theta
=\sum_i\frac{1}{\varepsilon_i^2}\,
\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{fit}}(k_i;\theta)}{\partial\theta}
\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{fit}}(k_i;\theta)}{\partial\theta}
^{\!\top},
\label{eq:infinfo}
\end{equation}
one outer product per quote, each weighted by that quote's
precision.  ($\sigma_{\mathrm{fit}}(k_i;\theta)$ is the model
volatility at quote $i$'s strike; the derivative is the row vector
of its sensitivities to the parameters.)  Why is the \emph{inverse}
of $\mathcal{I}_\theta$ the covariance of the fitted parameters?
The one-parameter case shows the whole mechanism with first-course
algebra.  Write the whitened quote equations as
$a_i\theta\approx b_i$, where $a_i$ is quote $i$'s sensitivity
divided by its noise and $b_i$ the whitened quote; least squares
solves them as $\hat\theta=\sum_ia_ib_i\big/\sum_ia_i^2$.  Each
$b_i$ is the true value plus unit-variance noise,
$b_i=a_i\theta_{\mathrm{true}}+\text{noise}_i$, so
\[
\hat\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{true}}
=\frac{\sum_ia_i\,\text{noise}_i}{\sum_ia_i^2},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\theta)
=\frac{\sum_ia_i^2\cdot1}{\bigl(\sum_ia_i^2\bigr)^2}
=\frac{1}{\sum_ia_i^2}
=\frac{1}{\mathcal{I}_\theta}:
\]
the estimation error is a noise-weighted average whose variance is
exactly one over the information.  With several parameters the same
computation, carried through with matrices, gives
$\mathcal{I}_\theta^{-1}$ as the covariance
\citep{SeberLee2003}.  Any handle $O(\theta)$ then inherits its
variance by first-order error propagation --- the delta method:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{obs}}(O)
=\frac{\partial O}{\partial\theta}^{\!\top}
\mathcal{I}_\theta^{-1}\,
\frac{\partial O}{\partial\theta}.
\label{eq:infdelta}
\end{equation}
In the one-strike warm-up, \cref{eq:infinfo} is the scalar
$n/\varepsilon^2$ and \cref{eq:infdelta} returns
$\varepsilon^2/n$: the general formula is the simple one with the
geometry of the strike grid kept.  (One guard on the inverse: a
parameter direction the quotes do not touch makes
$\mathcal{I}_\theta$ nearly singular, and a naive inverse would
assign that direction either an infinite variance --- harmless ---
or, after aggressive rounding, \emph{zero}, which is the one lie a
filter must never inherit; the reference implementation floors the
small eigenvalues so unidentified directions come out
large-but-finite.)

Two disciplines make \cref{eq:infdelta} honest in practice, and
\cref{fig:infcovar} measures both.

\emph{Units.}  Information is only information \emph{in stated
noise}.  If the fit's weights are merely relative --- equal weights,
say --- then \cref{eq:infinfo} silently prices every quote as if its
noise were one full volatility point, and the resulting
``covariance'' is fiction.  The whitening by the actual
$\varepsilon_i$ is what ties the budget to the market's own stated
uncertainty, and it implies a checkable contract: variance is noise
\emph{squared}, propagated.  Panel~(a) of \cref{fig:infcovar} checks
it on a synthetic \MacFiltCovarNq-quote chain, sweeping the stated
noise and recomputing \cref{eq:infdelta}: the ATM observation
standard deviation follows with log--log slope \MacFiltCovarSlope.
Double what the market says its quotes are worth and the computed
observation variance rises exactly fourfold --- by construction,
not by tuning.

\emph{Consistency.}  Geometry is not agreement.
Equations~\cref{eq:infinfo}--\cref{eq:infdelta} see \emph{where} the
quotes sit and how noisy each claims to be --- but not whether they
agree with one another.  The stale-strike morning of
\cref{sec:inffilter} is exactly that gap: plenty of quotes, fine
stated noise, and a cluster that cannot all be right at once.  The
fix reads the fit's own residuals.  After fitting, compute the
average of the squared whitened residuals --- each quote's misfit
divided by its stated noise, squared, averaged over the quotes
(less a small correction for the parameters fitted; the exact
expression is in Appendix~\ref{app:infprotocol}).  If the quotes
scatter as their stated noise claims, this average is close to one.
If it comes out at, say, eleven, the chain is misbehaving eleven
times worse than it admits --- and the observation variance is
multiplied by that realized-misfit multiple (floored at one, capped
so a single broken chain cannot poison the state).  Panel~(b) shows
the mechanism end to end: two adjacent strikes are kinked apart by a
growing amount, and the computed gains respond.  While the kink
hides inside the stated noise, the multiple sits at its floor and
nothing changes --- the flat start of both curves.  Beyond it, the
multiple climbs (it reaches \MacFiltCovarMultMax\ at a three-point
kink) and the curvature gain collapses from
\MacFiltCovarGainCurvClean\ to \MacFiltCovarGainCurvKinked, while
the level gain --- a handle this chain reads to
\MacFiltCovarBaseSdBp\ bp against a much wider prediction --- falls
far less, from \MacFiltCovarGainLevelClean\ to
\MacFiltCovarGainLevelKinked.  The one multiple distrusts the whole
chain, and it bites hardest where the observation was weakest
relative to its prediction.  No threshold fired and no rule named
``stale strike'': the contradiction raised the stated variance, and
the gain, a ratio of variances, turned itself.

\subsection{The prediction budget: how far can the smile have walked?}

The other variance belongs to the prediction, and it answers: how
much can this handle genuinely have changed since it was last
combined?  The reference implementation grows it additively,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}^{-}
=\mathcal{V}^{+}_{\mathrm{prev}}
+q_{\mathrm{walk}}^2\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{days}}
+(\text{widenings}),
\label{eq:infpred}
\end{equation}
starting from yesterday's posterior variance.  The second term is
the \emph{clock term}: surfaces diffuse, and a handle drifts about
$q_{\mathrm{walk}}$ per square-root day --- the square-root scaling
of any diffusion, Chapter~8's accrual logic applied to the handle
itself.  Two honesty points live inside it.  The elapsed time
$\Delta t_{\mathrm{days}}$ should be counted on the market's clock,
not the wall's: Chapter~8 measured that a closed weekend moves the
smile about as much as one overnight, so a calendar count pays three
days of variance for one overnight's worth of movement and no
single $q_{\mathrm{walk}}$ can then be right at every cadence.  And
the scale $q_{\mathrm{walk}}$ itself is an empirical commitment ---
the audit of \cref{sec:infaudit} is precisely the instrument that
convicts a wrong one.  The remaining widenings are event-shaped and
listed in words: a spot move of size $H$ adds transport uncertainty
in proportion to $\lvert H\rvert$ (the transported prediction leans
on Chapter~9's dial, which is itself a convention); a scheduled
event inside the interval widens the budget while it resolves; a
change of data source resets the state outright rather than
pretending continuity.

One last mechanism guards the tails of real life.  A genuine
five-point overnight jump is, under a $30$-bp-per-$\sqrt{\text{day}}$
budget, a surprise of tens of standard deviations; a filter that
believes its budget will crawl toward it for days.  The honest
repair is not a bigger everyday budget --- that would fail the
audit in the other direction --- but a \emph{surprise widening}:
when the standardized innovation
$\lvert\hat O_{\mathrm{obs}}-\hat O^{-}\rvert
/\sqrt{\mathcal{V}^{-}+\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{obs}}}$ exceeds a few
standard deviations, multiply $\mathcal{V}^{-}$ up until the
surprise reads as merely unusual, and let the gain rise toward the
data.  (A widening, not another gate: nothing here switches off ---
a variance is enlarged.)  Below its threshold the update is
untouched; and a \emph{contradictory} chain does not trip it,
because its own inflated $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ already
shrinks the standardized innovation --- the two honesty mechanisms
compose rather than compete.  \Cref{sec:infaudit} measures all of
this.
