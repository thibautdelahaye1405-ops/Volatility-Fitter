\section{How much it matters, by population}
\label{sec:deammatters}

The single most misquotable number in this subject is ``the effect of
de-Americanization'' with the population left unsaid.  On one and the
same expiry it is a few basis points, or hundreds, depending on which
quotes you average over --- and both numbers are true.  This section
measures both on the frozen gallery --- the snapshot's eight SPY
expiries --- and then writes the chapter's contract.

First, name the populations.  A fitting desk reads the
\emph{out-of-the-money side} of the board: puts below the forward,
calls at or above it.  The reason is worth one careful sentence,
because it is \emph{not} that the out-of-the-money option has more
vega --- at any one strike the call and the put have exactly the same
vega, since parity ties their prices by a volatility-free line.  It
is that the out-of-the-money leg packs that same vega into a small
price with a tight spread, while its in-the-money twin dilutes it
through a wider spread on a bigger price and, by parity, says nothing
the other leg has not already said.  The book's frozen chains follow
that convention.  So the
number that describes a fit's exposure is the wedge --- naive minus
de-Americanized implied volatility, quote by quote --- \emph{on the
out-of-the-money side}; the wedge on the discarded in-the-money side
is a stress exhibit about quotes the fit never touches.

\Cref{fig:deamgallery}(a) shows the fitted side of the running node,
quote by quote (\MacDeamGalSelN\ quotes; construction in
appendix~\ref{app:deamprotocol}).  Walk the two halves separately.
The calls (blue, $k\ge0$) sit within a few basis points of zero,
median $\MacDeamGalSelCallMedianBp$ vol bp --- and the sign deserves
a sentence: a \emph{negative} wedge cannot be a negative premium
(\cref{prop:deamdom} forbids one); it is the finite tree's
European-leg gap from \cref{sec:deamsub}'s budget, the numerical
floor of the method showing through where the true premium is
essentially zero.  The puts (orange, $k<0$) are the real payload:
a few basis points out in the wing, rising steadily toward the money
and reaching \MacDeamGalSelMaxBp\ vol bp just below $k=0$.  That
rise has a clean reason worth owning.  The fitting coordinate $k$
measures moneyness against the \emph{forward}, but the exercise
right compares strike against the \emph{spot} --- and between the
spot and the forward (\MacFwdLineSpotDollars\ to
$\MacDeamBowFResolved$ here) sits a band of strikes whose puts are
out of the money to the fit and already in the money to the holder:
real intrinsic today, real premium, at nearly full vega.  The two
coordinate systems disagree exactly there, and the wedge marks the
spot.  Median over the whole fitted node:
\MacDeamGalSelMedianBp\ vol bp --- small, but not small against the
sub-basis-point error budgets Part~I's fits run on.

Panel~(b) sweeps the whole SPY gallery, one point per expiry.  The
fitted-population median (filled circles) grows with maturity, from
\MacDeamGalShortMedianBp\ vol bp on the one-day board to
\MacDeamGalWorstMedianBp\ at the long end --- exactly the maturity
dependence the premium map of \cref{fig:deammap} shows, more
calendar for the rate to work on --- and the worst single fitted
quote (open circles) reaches \MacDeamGalWorstMaxBp\ vol bp, always
in the put band just described.  The orange square is the other
population: the running node's \emph{discarded} in-the-money puts.
Of those, \MacDeamGalItmDead\ sit on the intrinsic floor with no
volatility to read at all --- the same \MacDeamGalItmDead\
floor-bound puts whose pairs dropped out of \cref{sec:deamloop}'s
refit --- and
the \MacDeamGalItmN\ that do invert carry a median wedge of
\MacDeamGalItmMedianBp\ vol bp, up to \MacDeamGalItmMaxBp.  An order
of magnitude separates the two populations.  When someone quotes
``the de-Americanization effect'', ask which book they averaged
over; when this book quotes one, the population is in the sentence.

One last statement of scope before the contract: what the subtraction
leaves behind.  Each root \eqref{eq:deamroot} is solved strike by
strike --- no coupling across strikes --- and the clamp
\eqref{eq:deamhat} nudges each strike from one side only.  Nothing in
that construction \emph{enforces} coherence across strikes, and
Chapter~2 taught what coherence means here: the call curve convex in
strike, because its second derivative is the density.  On a liquid
board the incoherence injected by noise and clamping hides below the
quoted spread; on a sparse wing it can surface as a small
\emph{butterfly violation} --- a locally non-convex stretch of the
call curve, hence a patch of negative implied density --- handed to
the model layer as input.  The reference
implementation, when it repairs such a wing, does so under two
self-imposed rules that are really one rule --- \emph{a repair aimed
where the data is weak must be structurally unable to move where the
data is strong}: wings only, never the near-money core, and never
outside the quoted band.  This book's fitting chapters assumed
arbitrage-aware inputs; this is the sentence that says who cleans
them.

Step back to the book's thesis.  \emph{Validity} was the stopping
theory: dominance and Merton's theorem hold for every model, and no
inversion may return a negative premium or read volatility off an
exercise floor.  \emph{Information} was the geography --- the premium
identified off the plateau, unidentifiable on it --- and the measured
split of populations: a few basis points where the fit lives,
hundreds where it does not.  \emph{Convention} was everything inside
the subtraction: the lattice, the dividend calendar, the clamp, the
depth, the stated rate.  Chapter~6 fitted the observation's frame;
this chapter removed the one right the frame could not see.  Every
tree in it marched on calendar time $t$, while the smile's variance
kept its own clock $\tau$ --- two clocks this book has so far been
allowed to conflate, because its frozen data happens to agree on
both.  The next chapter is about the days when they disagree.
