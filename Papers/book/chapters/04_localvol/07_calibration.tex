\section{The objective and its derivatives}
\label{sec:lvcalib}

\subsection{The objective}

The pieces assemble into a bound-constrained least-squares problem over
the nodal variances:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta\in[v_{\rm lo},\,v_{\rm hi}]^{n_\theta}}
\;\tfrac12\Big(
\sum_{q}r_q(\theta)^{2}
\;+\;\lambda\,\big\|\Gamma(\theta-\theta_{\rm ref})\big\|^{2}
\;+\;\lambda_0\,\big\|\theta_{1,\cdot}-\theta_{2,\cdot}\big\|^{2}
\Big),
\label{eq:lvobjective}
\end{equation}
whose three blocks have each been argued for already.

The \emph{data block} compares the marched call $c_\theta$ at quote $q$'s
coordinates with the quoted one, in vega units:
$r_q=\big(c_\theta(\tau_q,y_q)-c_q\big)/\eta_q$, with $\eta_q$ one
volatility percent of the quote's vega, floored (the same device as
Chapter~2's vega floor $\eta$; this chapter's constant is in
\cref{app:lvnumerics}).
This is the residual design of \cref{sec:calibration} --- solve in price
space, measure in volatility space --- carried over;
when band edges rather than mids are trusted, the band objective of that
section applies verbatim.  The \emph{roughness block} is Tikhonov
regularization with a curvature seminorm: $\Gamma$ takes second divided
differences along strike within each maturity row and along time within
each strike column, normalized by the local vertex spacing so that a
tightly spaced money column is not penalized more than a wide wing one
(it reduces to the $(1,-2,1)$ stencil on a uniform grid).  It pulls toward
a reference sheet $\theta_{\rm ref}$ --- flat, at the median ATM variance,
in this chapter's protocol --- and it is the declared answer to
\cref{sec:lvinfo}: of the equivalence class the data allows, return the
smoothest member near the reference.  The \emph{front tie} at weight
$\lambda_0$ pins the $\tau=0$ row to the first quoted row, closing the
unidentified sub-front subspace.  Two further row types plug into the same
stack when their instruments are present, and are only named here: a
variance-swap quote adds one scalar residual per quoted expiry,
constraining the deep-put tail integral that vanillas cannot see
(Chapter~5 develops the integral, and it is exactly the instrument that
justifies releasing the wing slope $a$ of \cref{rem:lvhull}); and prior
structures from a previous fit enter as reference terms in the same
$\Gamma$-and-$\theta_{\rm ref}$ position (Chapter~10 develops when and how
much to trust them).

\subsection{Every derivative from the same factorization}

A least-squares solver lives on Jacobians, and a PDE-implicit pricing map
sounds like finite-difference territory: $n_\theta$ rebuilds of the march
per iteration.  It is not, for the same structural reason that served the
earlier chapters: the map from parameters to operator is \emph{linear}.
By \eqref{eq:lvp1}, $\mathcal L(\theta)$ is linear in $\theta$ with
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial\theta_\ell=\mathcal L[\mathcal H_\ell]$ ---
the stencil \eqref{eq:lvstencil} with the field $v$ replaced by the hat
function $\mathcal H_\ell$.

\begin{proposition}[Tangent: same factor, extra right-hand sides]
\label{prop:lvtangent}
The sensitivities $s^{n}_\ell=\partial U^{n}/\partial\theta_\ell$ obey
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M^{n+1}s^{n+1}_\ell
=s^{n}_\ell+\Delta\tau\,\mathcal L[\mathcal H_\ell]\,U^{n+1},
\qquad s^{0}_\ell=0,
\label{eq:lvtangent}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L[\mathcal H_\ell]\,U^{n+1}$ applies the boundary-inclusive
stencil to the solution extended by its fixed boundary values.  This is the
discretization of the continuous sensitivity equation
$\partial_\tau s_\ell=\tfrac12 v y^{2}\partial_{yy}s_\ell
+\tfrac12\mathcal H_\ell\,y^{2}\partial_{yy}c$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write one step over the interior unknowns as
$\mathcal M^{n+1}U^{n+1}=U^{n}+b^{n+1}$, where $b^{n+1}$ carries the
Dirichlet data through the stencil's boundary columns --- and therefore
depends on $\theta$ through the field at the boundary-adjacent nodes.
Differentiating in $\theta_\ell$ and using
$\partial_{\theta_\ell}\mathcal M^{n+1}
=-\Delta\tau\,\mathcal L[\mathcal H_\ell]$ (exact by linearity) gives
\eqref{eq:lvtangent}, the interior product
$\mathcal L[\mathcal H_\ell]U^{n+1}$ and the derivative of $b^{n+1}$
combining into the boundary-inclusive product, because the boundary values
themselves do not move with $\theta$.
\end{proof}

Read \eqref{eq:lvtangent} twice.  As a computation: every sensitivity
marches behind the \emph{same} tridiagonal factorization as the value
solve, so the entire $n_\theta$-column Jacobian costs one factorization
plus $n_\theta$ extra back-substitutions per step --- the columns form a
block of right-hand sides that a solver processes together.  As
probability: the source term says vertex $\ell$ influences a price exactly
through the diffusion's visits to $\ell$'s star of triangles, weighted by
the convexity it finds there.  \Cref{fig:lvinfluence} shows both readings
on the synthetic running example: the influence of one interior vertex is
a cone --- zero at the expiry \emph{before} the vertex's time, which the
march cannot reach backward; sharpest near its own maturity; spreading and
flattening as the diffusion forgets where it collected its variance ---
and every analytic column agrees with a central finite difference of the
full march to \MacLvInflMaxRel\ in relative terms.

The optimization itself is then standard machinery used plainly: a
trust-region reflective least-squares iteration on
\eqref{eq:lvobjective}, box bounds handled natively
\citep{BranchColemanLi1999}, the analytic Jacobian of
\cref{prop:lvtangent} supplied at every iterate, and a flat seed at the
reference sheet.  For the tangent route the Jacobian cost grows linearly
with $n_\theta$; the classical adjoint alternative --- one backward sweep
per objective, cost independent of the parameter count --- is derived in
\cref{app:lvnumerics} for the reader who will need it at larger grids.
