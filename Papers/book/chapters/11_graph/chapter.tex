\chapter[The graph: one surface from a sparse universe]{The Graph: One
  Surface from a Sparse Universe}
\label{ch:graph}
\chaptermark{The graph}

\chaptersynopsis{Everything so far has been one smile at a time: fit
it, check it, filter it.  But a desk does not own one smile --- it
owns a universe of them, one node per underlier and expiry, and on
any given morning only a handful are richly quoted (\emph{lit}, in
this chapter's vocabulary).  The rest still
need surfaces.  This final chapter completes the book's machinery by
letting the quoted nodes inform the unquoted ones.  The object is a
graph: nodes are the smiles, and each edge is a \emph{contract} ---
a statement that one node's move implies another's, at a stated
amplitude and a stated trust, and the two never mix: amplitude
prices the implied move, trust prices the band.  What the graph
infers is not levels but \emph{innovations} --- each node's departure
from its own transported baseline, Chapter~10's prior made
universal --- so the default answer everywhere is ``nothing changed,''
and lit data must earn every departure.  The whole-universe solve is
one least-squares objective, the same precision-weighted average as
Chapter~10's filter with many unknowns at once, and its accounting
can be defended line by line: trusting a neighbour that has nothing
to say costs a live message nothing, one
source is counted once however many routes carry it, and the
published confidence honestly includes the informers' own doubt.  A dark node's answer
then decomposes into three parts of decreasing evidence --- its
baseline, what the lit universe implies through the contracts, and
its own remembered dislocation, decaying on a stated half-life.  The
chapter ends where the book must: a twenty-node universe built from
the frozen boards, eight nodes lit, and the withheld December smile
of the second name reconstructed --- from its neighbours alone --- to
within a fraction of a volatility point of quotes it never saw, under
an error bar that an audit certifies.}
