\section{Less than full force: anchors and corroboration}
\label{sec:granchor}

The contracts so far transfer at full amplitude: one point at the
informer is $\beta$ points at the receiver, however far the message
travels.  That is the right semantics \emph{conditional on the
relation as asserted} --- but a desk often wants to assert less.
Related names do not, on average, transfer each other's moves one for
one; a desk wants a stated fraction through, with
``no move'' keeping some weight at the receiver.  The question of this
section is how to say ``believe it, but not at full force'' without
breaking anything already built.  Two natural answers are provably
wrong, and working through them is the fastest way to trust the third.

\subsection{Two wrong ways}

Suppose the desk wants \emph{half} the message through.

\emph{Wrong way one: scale the amplitude.}  Replace $\beta$ by
$\tfrac12\beta$ in the factor.  The forward reading now transfers
half, as intended.  But \cref{sec:gredge} showed that one factor
carries \emph{two} readings, and the reverse reading of
$p\,(\Theta_i-\tfrac12\beta\,\Theta_j)^{2}$ has amplitude
\[
\frac{1}{\tfrac12\beta}=\frac{2}{\beta}:
\]
twice the original reverse amplitude.  The shrink attenuates one
direction by \emph{amplifying} the other --- a receiver's print would
now move its informer at double force.  Attenuation is not a property
a single symmetric factor can have; scaling $\beta$ just relocates
the excess.

\emph{Wrong way two: two directed shrunk factors.}  Then write two
factors, one per direction, each shrunk by the same half: the forward
factor predicts the receiver at $\tfrac12\beta$ per informer point,
and the reverse factor predicts the informer at half of
\emph{its} reading, $\tfrac12\Theta_i/\beta$.  Hold the informer
fixed at $m$ (\emph{clamped}: treated as observed exactly, a
certified print), and give the reverse factor the units-matched
precision $p'=p\beta^{2}$ of \cref{sec:gredge} so that neither
direction is favoured by the units.  The receiver then minimizes
\[
p\,\bigl(\Theta_i-\tfrac12\beta m\bigr)^{2}
+p\beta^{2}\bigl(m-\tfrac12\Theta_i/\beta\bigr)^{2}.
\]
Set the derivative in $\Theta_i$ to zero:
\[
\bigl(\Theta_i-\tfrac12\beta m\bigr)
-\tfrac12\beta\bigl(m-\tfrac12\Theta_i/\beta\bigr)=0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\Theta_i\bigl(1+\tfrac14\bigr)=\beta m
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\Theta_i=\tfrac{4}{5}\,\beta m .
\]
Eighty percent through, not fifty --- and on top of the wrong number,
the relation has been entered twice, which \cref{sec:graccount}
already ruled out.  (The general shrink $s$ transfers
$2s/(1+s^{2})$; setting $2s/(1+s^{2})=s$ --- asking the configured
shrink to be the delivered one --- forces $s=\pm1$: full force, or a
sign flip.  No genuine shrink survives.)

\subsection{The anchor}

The consistent mechanization does not touch the relation at all.  It
adds a new belief \emph{about the node}: a zero-innovation
pseudo-observation --- ``absent evidence, this node does not move'' ---
at a stated precision $\kappa_i$.  This is the middle term of
\cref{eq:grjoint}, switched on at last, and it is Chapter~10's
device: yesterday entering the objective as one extra row
(\cref{sec:infgate}), here with the transported baseline itself as
the target, since $\Theta_i=0$ \emph{means} ``publish the baseline.''
With one clamped source sending message $m$ at precision $p$, the
receiver minimizes $p(\Theta_i-m)^{2}+\kappa_i\Theta_i^{2}$, and
completing the square gives
\[
\hat\Theta^{+}_i=\frac{p}{p+\kappa_i}\,m .
\]
Half through: set $\kappa_i=p$.  A quarter: $\kappa_i=3p$.  The
shrink lives in a \emph{node} belief, so it cannot break the
relation's reciprocity, cannot double-count it, and composes with
everything already proved.

What happens when several sources agree?  With $k$ clamped agreeing
sources, all at precision $p$ and amplitude one, rule
\cref{eq:grcond} says the messages average to their common value $m$
at conditional precision $kp$; the anchor then shrinks by the same
completed square:
\begin{equation}
\hat\Theta^{+}_i=\frac{kp}{kp+\kappa_i}\,m .
\label{eq:grcorr}
\end{equation}
Read \cref{eq:grcorr} at $\kappa_i=3p$: one source pushes a quarter
through; two sources, $2/5$; three, one half; corroboration keeps
climbing toward one.  Independent agreeing sources gain force
against the anchor --- which is how a cautious default coexists with
conviction when the whole neighbourhood moves together.
\Cref{fig:granchor} draws the law for three anchor settings, with the
chapter's solver run at each point --- the sources clamped, as the
law's hypothesis requires: the dots sit on the curves to
\MacGrAnchorLawGap.  (Unclamped sources carry their own doubt, and
the transfer then sits below these curves --- the marginal-variance
lesson of \cref{sec:grmeet}, unchanged.)  One subtlety needs stating:
$\kappa_i$ is \emph{fixed per node} --- one belief about the node,
sized off its primary relation (its highest-precision incident edge,
read in the node's own units) --- not rescaled as further edges
arrive.  Tie $\kappa_i$ proportionally to the node's total incoming
precision instead, and \cref{eq:grcorr}'s ratio freezes at its
one-source value: a legal prior, but one that flatly denies
corroboration.  Which behaviour a desk wants is a statement about the
world, and the fixed anchor --- corroboration lifts transfer --- is the
convention this book adopts.

The dial exists: amplitude still prices what a move
means, trust still prices how tightly a relation binds, and the
anchor prices how strongly a node holds to its baseline against its
neighbours' messages.  All three are separately stated numbers.
What none of them yet captures is \emph{time} --- a node
that was seen dislocated an hour ago, and has been dark since.  That
is the last ingredient.
