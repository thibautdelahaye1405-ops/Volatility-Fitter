\section{Computation: earning the theorems}
\label{sec:computation}

The propositions of \cref{sec:validity} are statements about continuous
objects.  An implementation prices through quadrature and interpolation,
and an interpolant inherits nothing automatically.  This section gives
the numerical analysis in full: the quadrature, the optimization charts,
a catalogue of the floating-point failure modes and their remedies, and
--- the section's organizing principle --- the \emph{certificates} by
which the discretization earns, rather than borrows, the continuous
theorems.

\subsection{The quadrature}

All integrals live on a fixed symmetric grid $z_j\in[-Z_{\max},Z_{\max}]$
in log-odds, with $Z_{\max}=40$ and $8001$ points (odd, so $z=0$ is a
node); optimization
iterates on a coarser $2001$-point grid and the accepted fit is rebuilt
once on the full grid (converged parameters agree to \MacTwoGridParamAgree\
between the two).  One build is five steps: evaluate $g$ at the cached
ranks (one matrix--vector product --- $g$ is linear in $\theta$, and the
Legendre matrix is cached); integrate $e^{g}$ cumulatively
(fourth-order Simpson) into $\bar{x}$, anchored at the median node;
normalize, $m=-\log I$; integrate $e^{x}\rho$ cumulatively from the
right into $G$; stop.  Nothing is iterative, and the exact nodal
derivatives $x'=e^{g\circ\Lambda}$ and $G'=-e^{x}\rho$ come free, so
off-grid evaluation uses cubic \emph{Hermite} interpolation --- locally
$O(h^{4})$ with no spline solve --- and the strike root $x(z_k)=k$ is
polished by Newton on the interpolant to machine precision, which is
what makes the ATM handles of \cref{prop:atm} exact rather than
grid-limited.

Truncation at $|z|=Z_{\max}$ is not prayed away but summed: beyond the
grid the integrands are exponentials to $O(e^{-Z_{\max}})$ relative
accuracy, so the build appends the closed-form tail corrections
\begin{equation}
\int_{Z_{\max}}^{\infty}e^{\bar{x}}\rho\,\dd z
\approx\frac{e^{\bar{x}(Z_{\max})-Z_{\max}}}{1-\lambda_+},
\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^{-Z_{\max}}e^{\bar{x}}\rho\,\dd z
\approx\frac{e^{\bar{x}(-Z_{\max})-Z_{\max}}}{1+\lambda_-},
\label{eq:tailcorr}
\end{equation}
(the right-hand denominator displaying the wall once more).  Strikes
beyond the grid's return range are priced on the slice's own tail
asymptote, and the formula is worth deriving in the two lines it takes,
because its $\lambda_+/(1-\lambda_+)$ factor looks mysterious until the
subtraction is seen.  For $k$ past the grid edge, the strike root
extrapolates linearly along the tail,
$z_R(k)=Z_{\max}+\big(k-x(Z_{\max})\big)/\lambda_+$; on that tail the
ledger entry and the cash leg of \eqref{eq:call} are both single
exponentials,
\[
G(z_k)\approx\frac{e^{\,k-z_R(k)}}{1-\lambda_+}
\quad\text{(the tail correction \eqref{eq:tailcorr} restarted at
$z_R(k)$, where $x= k$)},
\qquad
e^{k}\big(1-u_k\big)\approx e^{\,k-z_R(k)} ,
\]
and their difference gives
\begin{equation}
c(k)=e^{\,k-z_R(k)}\Big(\frac{1}{1-\lambda_+}-1\Big)
=e^{\,k-z_R(k)}\,\frac{\lambda_+}{1-\lambda_+}
\label{eq:beyondgrid}
\end{equation}
(mirrored on the left for puts): continuous at the seam with
\eqref{eq:tailcorr} and Lee-consistent by construction.  The asymptote is
not decoration --- the display edge of a short-dated smile genuinely
reaches it.

\subsection{Three charts, one optimum}
\label{sec:charts}

The optimizer may run in any of three coordinate systems on the same
family; the objective is identical, so the optimum is chart-independent
(verified numerically on live nodes: parameters agree across charts to
\MacChartEquivParamAgree), and the choice is purely about conditioning
and constraint geometry.

\emph{Raw:} $\theta=(L,R,a_2,\dots,a_N)$.  Simple, but body and tails are
coupled \eqref{eq:endpoints}, and the wall $\lambda_+<1$ is a boundary
the trust region must respect.

\emph{Endpoint:} $\phi=(\log\lambda_-,\log\lambda_+,a_2,\dots,a_N)$, with
$\theta=M\phi$ the exact unit-determinant linear back-solve of
\eqref{eq:endpoints}.  Equivalently, the body basis becomes the
endpoint-vanishing combinations $P_n(1-2u)-(1-u)-(-1)^{n}u$: same
polynomial space, different names on the axes.  Body moves now hold both
tail scales fixed \emph{by construction} --- the chart in which tail
priors, manual edits, and warm starts speak the language they mean.

\emph{Logistic} (production default): additionally set
$\lambda_+=\expit(\xi)$, $\xi\in\R$.  Every point of $\R^{N+1}$ is now
admissible: the wall is not respected but \emph{unreachable}, and the
word ``unconstrained'' in \cref{sec:validity} becomes a literal statement
about the production solve.  Near the wall the chart compresses ---
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd\log\lambda_+}{\dd\xi}=1-\lambda_+ ,
\label{eq:chartcompression}
\end{equation}
so trust-region steps shrink, in $\log\lambda_+$ terms, exactly where the
raw chart needed a barrier; far from the wall $\xi\approx\log\lambda_+$
and nothing changes.  One honest footnote: the logistic chart removes the
mathematical wall, not the floating-point one --- beyond
$\xi\approx36.7$, $\expit(\xi)$ rounds to exactly $1$ --- so the build
retains a hard admissibility guard $\lambda_+\le1-10^{-6}$ regardless of
chart.

\subsection{The floating-point catalogue}

Structural theorems do not survive IEEE-754 by themselves.  The
catalogue below is the complete list of the failure modes met in
practice, each with its remedy; every one of them was found either by
the randomized audit described next or by a production incident, and
each is now locked by a regression test.  (General background:
\citealp{Higham2002}.)

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Rank saturation.}  For $|z|\gtrsim36.7$ (i.e.\
$e^{-|z|}<2^{-53}$), $\expit(z)$ rounds to exactly $1$, so the factor
$1-u$ silently becomes $0$ and $u(1-u)$ becomes $0$.  Remedy: never form
$1-u$ from $u$; use $u(1-u)=\expit(z)\expit(-z)$ (each factor evaluated
on its own accurate side) and keep cash legs in log space,
$e^{k}(1-u_k)=\exp\!\big(k-\log(1+e^{z_k})\big)$ via a stable
log-sum-exp.  This failure was live once: the cash leg of
\eqref{eq:call} collapsed to zero in the far right wing and the priced
call stepped up to the bare ledger $G(z_k)$; the audit caught it as a
butterfly violation of order \MacAuditPreFixFly, and the log-space
remedy eliminates it --- the re-audit at the same strikes and stencil
finds a worst violation of exactly \MacAuditPostFixFly.
\item \emph{Interior overflow.}  Endpoint admissibility does not bound
the body: cancellation in \eqref{eq:endpoints} can keep $\lambda_\pm$
tame while an interior excursion of $g$ overflows $e^{g}$ or the running
integral.  Remedy: an explicit exponent budget --- the build refuses,
with a clean exception the calibrator's penalty branch absorbs, any
trial whose $g$ or unnormalized transport exceeds $700$ (just under
$\log$ of the double-precision ceiling, $\approx709.78$).  A guard at
the wall cannot substitute: it watches the endpoints, and this failure
lives in the middle.
\item \emph{The Lee map at large moment order.}  The textbook form
$\Psi(r)=2-4(\sqrt{r^{2}+r}-r)$ subtracts two nearly equal large numbers
and, for $r\gtrsim10^{15}$, returns $\approx2$ where the truth is
$\approx0$.  Remedy: rationalize once,
$\sqrt{r^{2}+r}-r=r\big(\sqrt{1+1/r}-1\big)=1/\big(\sqrt{1+1/r}+1\big)$,
giving the algebraically identical
\begin{equation}
\Psi(r)=2-\frac{4}{\sqrt{1+1/r}+1}\,,
\label{eq:leestable}
\end{equation}
which subtracts nothing large and is accurate over the whole range.  Companion guards:
$\lambda\to0$ maps to $\beta=0$ (the finite limit, not a division
error), and $\lambda_+\ge1$ reports the ceiling $\beta_R=2$.
\item \emph{Degenerate quadrature inputs.}  The parameter-independent
grid arrays are cached and returned read-only, so an accidental in-place
write errors instead of corrupting every later build --- a hygiene rule,
recorded because the failure it prevents is silent and global.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Certificates: the discretization earns the theorem}
\label{sec:certificates}

\cref{prop:noarb} is about the continuous slice.  The implementation
prices through two Hermite interpolants, and ordinary cubic Hermite
pieces are \emph{not} monotonicity-preserving in general: positive nodal
derivatives do not preclude segment overshoot.  The honest guarantee is
therefore a pair: a structural theorem for the continuous object, plus a
\emph{certificate} and a measured tolerance for the implementation.
Both are cheap; both run in production; neither is taken on faith.

\emph{The monotonicity certificate.}  For a cubic Hermite interpolant of
an increasing function on a grid, the Fritsch--Carlson condition
\citep{FritschCarlson1980} is sufficient: on each segment, monotonicity
holds if both endpoint derivatives lie in $[0,\,3\times\text{secant
slope}]$.  With exact nodal derivatives of a smooth curve the
derivative-to-secant ratio is $1+O(h^{2})$, far inside the region, so
the scan passes with margin --- and the \emph{margin} (the worst
distance to the boundary of the Fritsch--Carlson box, over all active
segments) is the certified quantity, reported per slice.  Segments where
the ledger has decayed below round-off are certified
flat-to-tolerance rather than monotone; the caveat is part of the
certificate, not a footnote to it.

\emph{The belly certificate.}  Convexity of the priced smile over the
traded range is re-checked from the model's own derivatives: the
function $g_{\mathrm{D}}$ of \eqref{eq:durrleman}, evaluated at $801$
points across the quoted strike range from analytic $w$, $w'$, $w''$
(never from differenced prices), must clear $-10^{-4}$.  The threshold
has stated units because \eqref{eq:durrleman} is an exact
factorization: $g_{\mathrm{D}}$ is normalized so a flat smile gives
$g_{\mathrm{D}}\equiv1$, and a reading of $-10^{-4}$ tolerates a
density no more negative than $10^{-4}$ of the local Black density
factor $\phin(d_-)/\sqrt{w}$ --- a relative tolerance, not an
unexplained constant.  The grid is
dense because the classical counterexamples are narrow --- a known
arbitrageable SVI parameter set dips to only $-0.033$ over a width of
$\approx0.02$ in $k$ --- and the check costs \MacBellyCertMs, so it runs
on every publish and a failure blocks it.

\emph{The randomized audit.}  What certificates do not cover ---
convexity at finite butterfly widths, digital bounds, grid adequacy ---
a battery measures.  \MacAuditSliceCount\ parameter vectors are drawn
through the logistic chart (hence all admissible by construction) with
a fixed seed, stratified across orders \MacCertOrdersList\ and three
stress classes --- plain, near-wall, and wild-body --- with equal
draws per cell.  Each slice is priced at randomized sub-grid strikes
and audited \emph{in the strike variable} $y=e^{k}$ --- convexity lives
there, per \cref{prop:noarb}(iv), and checking it in $k$ was the
battery's own first bug.  Worst violations across the battery:
call-price bounds \MacAuditBoundsWorst, butterflies \MacAuditFlyWorst,
digital bounds \MacAuditDigitalWorst, and agreement with a $4\times$
finer reference quadrature at random sub-grid strikes
\MacAuditGridAgreeWorst\ --- one tolerance set, uniform across the
classes.  The quotable summary: \emph{the implementation's residual
arbitrage is bounded by the worst figures just quoted, in normalized
price, across \MacAuditSliceCount\ adversarially drawn slices} --- which
is the sense
in which ``cannot have arbitrage'' becomes ``measured not to, at
tolerance so-and-so''.
