\section{Limitations, and the contract}
\label{sec:limits}

A model is used safely in proportion to how precisely its limits are
stated.  This closing section collects everything the paper does
\emph{not} claim, most of it already flagged in place, and ends with the
contract table.

\emph{What the family cannot represent.}  Per \cref{prop:universal}: no
atoms (a pinned settlement print, a tender-offer spike), no bounded
support, no interior zero-density gaps, no default mass at zero, no
Gaussian or faster tails.  All of these are \emph{smoothed}, not
represented; the double-hump example shows the smoothing is graceful and
order-controlled, but graceful smoothing of a genuine atom is still a
misstatement of the law, and a desk pricing digitals across a known pin
should know it.

\emph{Tails are priors.}  The two tail coordinates are weakly identified
by any realistic strip (\cref{sec:tails}).  The model's virtue is not
that it extracts tail information others miss --- it is that its tail
convention is two readable numbers, Lee-consistent by construction,
overridable by policy, and honestly separable from what the quotes pin.
Reported values of $\lambda_\pm$ and $\beta_\pm$ should travel with the
fitting recipe that selected them.

\emph{Calendar enforcement is a control.}  \Cref{thm:ledgerorder} is
exact for the continuous slices; production enforcement is
finite-grid, finite-weight, and confined to the common quoted support
(\cref{sec:calcontrol}).  Outside that support, ordering between
extrapolations is reported, not enforced; the illustration on the two
shortest SPY expiries is the standing example of why this is the right
scope and of what the diagnostic then shows.

\emph{The continuous theorems are earned, not inherited.}  Every
guarantee in \cref{sec:validity} passes to the implementation only
through the certificates and the audit of \cref{sec:computation}, whose
worst measured violations are the real numbers a risk committee should
file.  A reimplementation from this paper inherits the theorems and
none of the certificates until it runs them.

\emph{Single-expiry statics only.}  The model prices one marginal law
per expiry.  It says nothing about dynamics --- no spot-vol correlation
process, no forward smile, no jump identification --- and the var-swap
identity \eqref{eq:varswap} inherits the log contract's idealizations.
Coupling to dynamics, and joint fits across underliers, are outside the
paper.

\emph{Performance claims are scoped.}  Timings are medians with IQRs
from one machine and one interleaved protocol (\cref{tab:timing});
the portable claim is the complexity accounting, not the milliseconds.

The design thesis, restated once in closing.  Validity, information,
and convention are different kinds of statement, and a fitter's
architecture should keep them different: validity belongs in the
coordinates, where it cannot be traded away by an optimizer under
pressure; information belongs in audits, where it is measured rather
than presumed; convention belongs in daylight, where it can be argued
about.  The LQD construction is one demonstration that the separation
is achievable at production cost --- one array, five uses --- for the
class of smiles that equity markets actually quote.
