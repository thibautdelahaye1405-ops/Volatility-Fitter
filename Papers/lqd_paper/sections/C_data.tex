\section{Data and reproducibility}
\label{app:data}

\subsection{The frozen snapshot}

Every real-market number and figure in the paper derives from one frozen
JSON artifact, captured once and never refetched:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Source}: a delayed consolidated US listed-options feed;
after-hours book of the Monday 2026-08-03 US session (reference date
2026-08-03, capture timestamp 2026-08-04 02:08 UTC).
\item \emph{Universe}: SPY (spot \MacSpySpot) and NVDA (spot
\MacNvdaSpot), eight expiries each, 2026-08-05 through 2027-12-17 ---
maturities from \MacNvdaOneDayDays\ calendar days to
\MacNvdaLongTYears\ years.  \MacSnapNodeCount\ fitted nodes in total.
\item \emph{Fit recipe} (stamped in the file's manifest): LQD at order
$N=16$ under the guard \eqref{eq:orderguard}, logistic chart, haircut
target \eqref{eq:haircut} with $h=0.005$, equal quote weights, ridge
$\alpha=10^{-6}$, $\nu=1$, calendar rows \eqref{eq:calrows} active.  No
per-node tuning of any kind.
\item \emph{Quality}: all lit nodes fitted and ready; median quote-fit
rms \MacSnapMedianRmsBp\ vol bp, worst \MacSnapWorstRmsBp\ vol bp (the
shortest NVDA node).
\item \emph{Self-containment}: the file embeds the normalized chains,
per-node prepared quotes with forward and discount provenance, fitted
parameters, and fitted curves --- everything needed to re-run any fit or
re-price any figure offline, with no feed access.
\end{itemize}

One family-level advisory is disclosed rather than suppressed, because
\cref{sec:calendar} uses it as a live illustration: a full-grid
\emph{vol-space} calendar audit at equal log-moneyness (both legs read
on the far node's clock) flags a \MacCalGapVolBp\ vol bp crossing
between the two shortest SPY expiries --- two and four calendar days
from the reference date --- at $k=\MacCalGapArgmaxK$, far outside the
pair's common quote span $[\MacCalSpanLo,\MacCalSpanHi]$, where the
call prices are \MacCalendarCallShort\ and \MacCalendarCallLong\ of
forward (bounded by \MacCalWingPriceOrder).  A price-space audit over
all adjacent pairs on the full display grid finds a worst violation of
\MacCalPriceGridWorst\ of forward --- round-off.  Per-node quality is
clean; the crossing lives entirely in the extrapolated region that
\cref{sec:calcontrol} deliberately declines to constrain.

\subsection{Synthetic benchmarks}

Two synthetic targets appear in \cref{sec:examples}.  The
\emph{double-hump} target is a two-component lognormal mixture in the
gross return, martingale-normalized, with component weights, centers,
and widths fixed in the figure-generation script and printed in the
figure captions' companion macro file; it is quoted as noiseless implied
volatilities on a realistic strike ladder, so it measures approximation
capacity, not market fit.  The \emph{constant-speed} audit needs no
target data at all: it compares the implementation against the closed
forms of \cref{sec:constspeed}.

\subsection{Regeneration}

All figures and every data-derived number in the text (as LaTeX macros)
are produced by a single generator script that reads only the frozen
snapshot and the synthetic specifications --- never the network.  Real
nodes are rebuilt from their frozen coefficient vectors, not refitted,
and the rebuild is gated: the rebuilt display curves reproduce the
frozen ones to \MacRebuildWorstBp\ vol bp worst-case across all
\MacSnapNodeCount\ nodes, so every figure prices off exactly the
published fits.
Regenerating the paper is: run the generator (one pass, deterministic,
fixed seeds for the audit battery), then compile.  Timing tables are the
exception to determinism in value but not in protocol: they rerun the
interleaved median/IQR measurement of \cref{sec:computation} on the
build machine, and are expected to differ across machines --- the text
quotes them only with that caveat.  The snapshot file itself is the
citable artifact: refetching from the feed would produce a different
book and different (if statistically similar) numbers, and no claim in
the paper depends on which day was frozen.
