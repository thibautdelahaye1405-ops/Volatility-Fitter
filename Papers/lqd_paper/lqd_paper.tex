\title{\textbf{The Log-Quantile-Density Model of the Volatility Smile:\\
Validity, Information, and Convention}}
\author{Thibaut Delahaye}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A volatility smile is a probability distribution read through the Black
formula, and a smile fitter must manage three separable concerns: that its
output is a genuine distribution (validity), that the fitted curve reflects
what the quotes actually determine (information), and that everything the
quotes do not determine is chosen openly (convention).  Most parametrizations
entangle the three.  This paper develops a representation that separates
them.  The model writes the log-forward return as a monotone transport of a
logistic variable, with the logarithm of the transport speed a polynomial in
the rank; equivalently, it models the log quantile density as two universal
boundary terms plus a Legendre expansion.  Validity becomes structural: every
point of the open parameter set prices from a positive density with unit
mean, the single arbitrage condition being one inequality, $\lambda_+<1$,
which a logistic reparametrization removes entirely.  The two endpoint
values of the expansion are the exponential tail scales; they fix the exact
strip of finite moments and, through Lee's moment formula, the asymptotic
wing slopes in closed form.  One computational object carries the model: the
upper-share ledger $G(z)=\int_z^\infty e^{x(t)}\rho(t)\,\dd t$.  Pricing is
two ledger entries; the parameter Jacobian follows from an exact
envelope-cancellation identity on the same array; the risk-neutral density
is one division; the variance-swap strike is the first moment of the same
build; and absence of calendar arbitrage between expiries is the pointwise
ordering of two ledgers, proved both ways by conjugate duality.  The paper
is equally explicit about what is not claimed: the coordinates are
unconstrained for a flexible exponential-tail class, not for all smiles;
fitted tail parameters are priors selected jointly by basis, ridge, and
endpoint coupling, not observations; calendar enforcement is a control, not
a theorem; and the continuous theorems are earned by the discretization only
through explicit certificates and a randomized audit whose worst violations
are measured, not assumed.  All market examples are fitted from one frozen
snapshot of listed equity options, and every reported number regenerates
from that file.
\end{abstract}
